# Step-by-Step Reasoning

## 1. Direct Effects of the 100 bps Policy Rate Shock

**Immediate Monetary Tightening:**
- The unexpected 100 bps rate increase represents a significant tightening of monetary conditions
- At Q60, the policy rate was 1.7%; this shock would raise it to 2.7%
- This is a notably restrictive move given recent inflation has been moderating (1.4% in Q60, down from 2.6% in Q59)

**Direct Impact Channels:**
- **Output Gap**: Tighter monetary policy reduces aggregate demand through higher borrowing costs, reducing consumption and investment. This contracts the output gap (makes it more negative or less positive).
- **Inflation**: Higher rates directly reduce demand-pull inflation. However, the transmission lag is typically 2-4 quarters, so Q1 ahead effects are modest.
- **Unemployment**: Reduced demand weakens labor market demand, pushing unemployment upward, though again with a lag.

## 2. Second-Order Effects

**Exchange Rate Channel:**
- Higher policy rates relative to trading partners attract capital inflows
- The vantra appreciates (consistent with Q49-Q60 pattern of appreciation)
- Currency appreciation reduces export competitiveness and import costs
- This creates disinflationary pressure through import price channels (a mechanism evident in the historical data, particularly Q13-Q24)

**Monetary Policy Transmission:**
- The shock is unexpected, suggesting markets will revise growth expectations downward
- Financial conditions tighten beyond the rate increase itself (wider spreads, reduced credit availability)
- The lag structure is critical: most inflation effects occur 2-4 quarters ahead; unemployment effects occur 1-3 quarters ahead

**Labor Market Dynamics:**
- Weaker demand reduces hiring and increases layoffs with a 1-2 quarter lag
- At Q60, unemployment was already at 5.6% (above the 5.0% natural rate), suggesting some slack
- The labor market is positioned to absorb the demand shock with rising unemployment

## 3. Dynamics Over the 1-Quarter Forecast Horizon

**Q1 (Q61) Outcomes:**

**Inflation at Q1:**
- The 100 bps shock is unexpected and will be partially incorporated into expectations
- However, the direct pass-through to inflation is slow (Phillips curve effects)
- Recent inflation momentum shows volatility but is moderating (1.4% in Q60)
- The appreciation of the vantra will reduce import costs, supporting disinflation
- With only 1 quarter transmission lag, the effect is modest but directional
- **Expectation**: Inflation slightly decelerates from 1.4%, likely to 1.0-1.3%

**Output Gap at Q1:**
- The shock is very recent (contemporaneous with Q60/Q61 boundary)
- Real activity responds with a lag; most contraction occurs in Q2-Q3
- However, forward-looking agents may reduce spending in anticipation
- The output gap was positive (economy running above potential per Q49-Q60 narrative)
- At 1-quarter ahead, the gap begins to narrow but hasn't fully closed
- **Expectation**: Output gap contracts from positive territory toward zero/slightly negative

**Unemployment at Q1:**
- Labor market effects typically lag by 1-2 quarters
- At Q60, unemployment was 5.6% (above natural rate of 5.0%)
- Firms may reduce hiring immediately, but layoffs take time
- The most significant unemployment rise occurs in Q2-Q3
- At Q1, unemployment likely edges up modestly
- **Expectation**: Unemployment rises from 5.6% to approximately 5.7-5.9%

## Historical Context for Calibration

Looking at past policy tightening episodes:
- **Q37-Q42**: Policy tightening (1.5% to 2.9%) coincided with inflation rising from 0.4% to 3.2%, with unemployment falling from 3.9% to 5.4%
- **Q49-Q60**: Policy easing (1.7% declining to 1.7%) during disinflationary period with unemployment softening from 5.5% to 5.6%

The current shock is:
- **Larger and more unexpected** than gradual adjustments in the past
- **Countercyclical** (tightening despite moderating inflation and above-natural unemployment)
- **Likely to trigger significant market repricing**

## Forecast Uncertainty

Key sources of uncertainty:
1. **Expectations anchoring**: If expectations remain anchored to 2% target, inflation effects are muted
2. **Exchange rate overshooting**: The vantra could appreciate sharply, amplifying disinflationary pressure
3. **Financial conditions**: Credit conditions could tighten more than the rate alone suggests
4. **Forward guidance credibility**: The unexpectedness suggests markets may question central bank's reaction function
