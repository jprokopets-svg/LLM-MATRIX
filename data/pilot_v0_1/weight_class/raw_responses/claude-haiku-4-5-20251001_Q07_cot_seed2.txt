# Macroeconomic Forecast for the Republic of Vantria

## Step-by-Step Reasoning

### 1. Direct Effects of the Commodity Price Shock

**Inflation Channel:**
- The commodity price spike directly adds 1.5 percentage points to consumer price inflation this quarter
- Current inflation (Q60) is 1.4% year-on-year
- Direct impact: 1.4% + 1.5% = **2.9% inflation at Q61**

**Output Gap & Real Interest Rate:**
- A commodity price shock is a negative supply shock that raises input costs
- In the short run (1 quarter ahead), this primarily affects inflation through import/input prices
- Output effects depend on demand responses and policy reaction
- The real interest rate (nominal rate - expected inflation) will be affected by both the inflation shock and any monetary policy response

### 2. Second-Order Effects

**Monetary Policy Response:**
- Current nominal policy rate: 1.7% (Q60)
- The central bank targets 2.0% inflation
- With inflation rising to ~2.9%, this exceeds the target by 0.9 percentage points
- Historical pattern: The central bank responds gradually to inflation deviations (see Q37-Q48 tightening cycle)
- However, the shock is explicitly described as supply-driven, not demand-driven
- The central bank may recognize this as temporary and avoid aggressive tightening
- **Expected policy rate at Q61: likely hold or modest increase to ~1.8-1.9%** (cautious approach given supply shock nature)

**Exchange Rate Channel:**
- Recent pattern (Q49-Q60): vantra appreciated sharply
- A negative supply shock typically causes currency depreciation as investors reassess growth
- Depreciation would amplify import price pressures, reinforcing inflation
- However, this is a global commodity shock (affects all economies), limiting relative currency movement

**Labor Market & Demand:**
- Current unemployment: 5.6% (Q60, above natural rate of 5.0%)
- Labor market has softened recently
- A supply shock with slack labor market suggests limited wage-price spiral risk
- Output gap appears slightly negative or near-zero based on unemployment being above natural rate

**Real Interest Rate:**
- Nominal rate expected: ~1.8% (modest hold/increase)
- Expected inflation: ~2.9%
- Real rate: 1.8% - 2.9% = **-1.1%** (significantly negative)
- This is expansionary in real terms despite nominal tightening

### 3. Dynamics Over the Forecast Horizon (1 Quarter Ahead)

At 1 quarter ahead (Q61):
- **Inflation:** The commodity shock is fully realized. At 2.9%, it's above target but the nature of the shock (supply-driven, temporary) suggests central bank patience. No second-round effects yet given labor market slack.
- **Output Gap:** The negative supply shock reduces potential output slightly, but demand remains intact. With unemployment at 5.6% (above natural rate), there's slack. Output gap likely slightly negative or near zero: **-0.5% to 0.0%**
- **Real Interest Rate:** With nominal rates held relatively steady and inflation spiking, real rates turn sharply negative: **-1.0% to -1.2%**

### 4. Key Uncertainties

- **Central bank reaction function:** Will they tighten aggressively or acknowledge supply shock nature? Historical evidence suggests measured response.
- **Pass-through:** How much of commodity shock transmits to consumer prices? Not all commodity shocks fully pass through.
- **Exchange rate adjustment:** Depreciation could amplify inflation.
- **Duration:** Is this truly temporary (1-2 quarters) or more persistent?

## Final Predictions

### Inflation Rate at Q61 (1 quarter ahead)
The direct commodity shock of 1.5 percentage points adds to the current 1.4% baseline.
- **Point estimate: 2.9%**
- **80% CI: [2.5%, 3.3%]**
  - Lower bound reflects possible incomplete pass-through or measurement factors
  - Upper bound reflects potential exchange rate depreciation amplification

### Output Gap at Q61 (1 quarter ahead)
Recent unemployment at 5.6% is above the natural rate of 5.0%, suggesting slight negative output gap. The supply shock reduces potential output marginally but near-term demand remains intact. The negative supply shock combined with labor market slack suggests a near-zero to slightly negative gap.
- **Point estimate: -0.3%**
- **80% CI: [-1.0%, 0.5%]**
  - Lower bound: if supply shock is more severe than expected
  - Upper bound: if demand remains resilient and slack is quickly absorbed

### Real Interest Rate at Q61 (1 quarter ahead)
With nominal policy rate expected at ~1.8% and inflation spiking to 2.9%, the real rate becomes sharply negative.
- **Point estimate: -1.1%**
- **80% CI: [-1.5%, -0.5%]**
  - Lower bound: if inflation reaches 3.3% and rates held at 1.8%
  - Upper bound: if inflation stays at 2.5% or rates rise to 2.0%
