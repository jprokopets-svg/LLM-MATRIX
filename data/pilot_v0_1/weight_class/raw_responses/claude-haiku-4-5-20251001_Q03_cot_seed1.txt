# Macroeconomic Analysis: Vantria Rate Shock

## Step-by-Step Reasoning

### 1. Direct Effects of the 25bp Rate Increase (vs. 25bp surprise ease)

**Current Context (Q60):**
- Inflation: 1.4% (below 2% target)
- Policy rate: 1.7%
- Unemployment: 5.6% (above natural rate of 5.0%)
- Real interest rate: ~0.3% (nominal 1.7% - inflation 1.4%)
- Exchange rate: appreciating trend

**The Shock:**
The market priced in a 50bp increase, but the central bank delivered only 25bp. This is a **25bp negative surprise** (easier than expected), not a tightening shock. The central bank is signaling:
- Concern about below-target inflation (1.4% in Q60)
- Labor market softening (unemployment 5.6%)
- Economic slack despite "above-potential" narrative

**Direct impacts:**
- **Real interest rate**: Falls from ~0.3% to approximately 0.0-0.1% (nominal 1.95% if we assume the 25bp hike, minus expected inflation)
- **Exchange rate**: The negative surprise (easier than expected) should cause **depreciation** as the interest rate differential with trading partners narrows
- **Inflation**: No direct immediate impact, but the surprise ease signals future accommodation

### 2. Second-Order Effects

**Exchange Rate Channel (Primary):**
- Historical pattern: Vantria exhibits significant exchange rate volatility (sharp appreciation in Years 1, 10-12, 13-15; sharp depreciation in Years 4-6, 7-9)
- A 25bp surprise ease reduces the attractiveness of VTR assets relative to foreign alternatives
- Expected **real depreciation of 1-2%** in the near term
- This depreciation should support inflation through import price channels (as observed in Q18-Q24)

**Monetary Policy Expectations Channel:**
- Market had priced 50bp; only 25bp delivered = dovish surprise
- This signals the central bank is concerned about growth/inflation trade-off
- Forward guidance likely to emphasize "data-dependent" approach
- Expectations of future rate cuts may increase slightly

**Demand/Labor Market Channel:**
- Current unemployment is 5.6% (above natural rate), indicating slack
- Lower real rates support demand and employment
- With inflation below target, this accommodation is warranted
- Lag effects: employment impacts typically emerge over 2-3 quarters

**Inflation Dynamics:**
- Q60 inflation at 1.4% is 60bp below target
- The depreciation channel should add ~0.1-0.2% to inflation over next 2-3 quarters
- Labor market slack limits wage pressures in near term
- Inflation likely to move toward 1.6-1.8% range by Q61

### 3. Dynamics Over the 1-Quarter Forecast Horizon

**At Q61 (1 quarter ahead):**

**Inflation (π₁):**
- Base effect: 1.4% (Q60 carries forward with 1-quarter lag)
- Depreciation effect: +0.05-0.15% (partial pass-through in first quarter)
- Demand effect: minimal at 1-quarter horizon
- **Expected range: 1.5-1.7%**, point estimate: **1.6%**

**Real Exchange Rate (e₁):**
- The 25bp surprise ease causes immediate depreciation
- Historical volatility suggests 1-2% moves are plausible
- No offsetting appreciation pressure (growth narrative weakening)
- Real depreciation of **-1.2% to -1.8%** from current level
- **Point estimate: -1.5%** (log index change)

**Real Interest Rate (r₁):**
- Nominal rate rises to ~1.95% (1.7% + 0.25bp hike)
- Expected inflation rises to ~1.6%
- Real rate = 1.95% - 1.6% = **0.35%**
- Confidence interval accounts for uncertainty in inflation pass-through
- **Range: 0.15% to 0.50%**, point estimate: **0.35%**
