# Macroeconomic Forecasting Analysis: Vantria Rate Cut Scenario

## Step-by-Step Causal Analysis

### 1. Direct Effects of 50 bps Rate Cut

**Output Gap:**
- A 50 bps cut reduces the real policy rate, lowering borrowing costs
- This stimulates aggregate demand through investment and consumption channels
- Direct effect: positive shock to output gap (narrowing or widening depending on baseline)

**Real Exchange Rate:**
- Lower VTR rates reduce returns on vantra-denominated assets
- Capital outflows increase, depreciating the nominal exchange rate
- With sticky prices in the short term, real depreciation follows
- Direct effect: real exchange rate depreciates (log index declines)

**Unemployment Rate:**
- Higher aggregate demand from monetary stimulus reduces unemployment
- Direct effect: unemployment falls

### 2. Second-Order Effects

**Monetary Policy Transmission:**
- The rate cut operates with standard lags (typically 2-4 quarters for full effect)
- At 1 quarter ahead, effects are primarily direct with limited multiplier propagation
- The economy is described as "running well above potential" at Q60, suggesting limited slack
- This reduces the potency of demand stimulus

**Exchange Rate Feedback:**
- Real depreciation improves export competitiveness
- However, at 1 quarter ahead, this effect is nascent
- Import prices rise with depreciation, creating mild inflationary pressure
- This constrains the central bank's ability to cut further

**Labor Market Dynamics:**
- Unemployment at Q60 was 5.6%, above the 5.0% natural rate
- Recent labor market softening suggests cyclical weakness despite "above-trend growth"
- The rate cut should provide modest relief, but with a lag
- Expectations of future stimulus may cause some immediate adjustment

### 3. Dynamics Over Forecast Horizon (1 Quarter Ahead)

**At 1 Quarter Ahead:**

Looking at the recent history (Q49-Q60):
- Output gap was positive but volatile (above potential growth with inflation fluctuations)
- Unemployment has drifted toward natural rate (5.6% at Q60 vs. 5.0% NAIRU)
- Inflation has been volatile (1.4%-2.6% range), recently elevated at 2.6% in Q59, declining to 1.4% in Q60

**Estimated Starting Conditions (Q60 baseline):**
- Output gap: approximately +1.0% to +1.5% (economy above potential)
- Real exchange rate: near recent highs (vantra appreciated sharply)
- Unemployment: 5.6%

**One Quarter Forward Response:**

**Output Gap:**
- Monetary stimulus takes time to transmit (typically 2-4 quarters)
- At 1 quarter ahead, the direct demand effect is modest
- However, forward-looking behavior and expectations may bring some improvement
- Given the economy is already above potential, the gap may narrow slightly from stimulus
- Estimate: +0.8% to +1.2% (modest narrowing from stimulus, but still positive)

**Real Exchange Rate:**
- The 50 bps cut immediately reduces relative returns on VTR assets
- Capital outflows cause depreciation within 1 quarter
- Real depreciation of 1-2% is plausible at 1 quarter ahead
- Given recent appreciation, this represents a reversal
- Estimate: -1.5% to -2.5% depreciation from current levels

**Unemployment Rate:**
- Labor market lags are typically 2-3 quarters for full effect
- At 1 quarter ahead, the effect is minimal
- Expectations of stimulus may cause slight hiring improvement
- However, the recent softening (5.6% at Q60) suggests slack is emerging
- Modest improvement expected: 5.3% to 5.5%

## Specific Contextual Considerations

1. **Inflation at 1.4% in Q60** is below target, reducing concerns about overstimulation
2. **Recent unemployment rise to 5.6%** suggests cyclical weakness despite narrative of "above-trend growth"
3. **Vantra appreciation** suggests external strength that may be reversing with the rate cut
4. **Small open economy** dynamics mean exchange rate pass-through is significant
5. **Policy rate cut from ~1.7%** is meaningful in an economy with low rates
