# Macroeconomic Forecasting Analysis: Republic of Vantria

## Step-by-Step Causal Reasoning

### 1. Direct Effects of the 25bp Rate Increase Shock

**Current Conditions (Q60 baseline):**
- Policy rate: 1.7%
- Inflation: 1.4% (below 2% target)
- Unemployment: 5.6% (above natural rate of 5.0%)
- Real interest rate: ~0.3% (1.7% - 1.4%)
- Exchange rate: appreciating trend

**Shock Specification:**
The market expected a 50bp increase, but the central bank delivered only 25bp. This is a **dovish surprise** (easier than expected), not a tightening shock in absolute terms.

**Direct effects at 1-quarter horizon:**
- **Policy rate**: Rises from 1.7% to 1.95% (+25bp)
- **Real interest rate**: Increases from ~0.3% to ~0.55% (assuming inflation sticky in short term)
- **Exchange rate**: The dovish surprise relative to market expectations should weaken the vantra initially, as carry traders and foreign investors reassess Vantria's relative attractiveness

### 2. Second-Order Effects

**Monetary Policy Channel:**
- The 25bp increase is restrictive in absolute terms but accommodative relative to market pricing
- The economy is running "above potential" per the narrative, but inflation is below target (1.4%)—suggesting supply-side strength or demand weakness
- This mixed signal means the rate increase is moderately contractionary but calibrated to avoid excessive tightening
- With unemployment at 5.6% (above natural rate), the tighter policy should gradually restore slack

**Exchange Rate Channel:**
- The vantra has been appreciating sharply (noted in Q49-Q60 period)
- A smaller-than-expected rate increase reduces the interest rate differential vs. trading partners
- This should dampen appreciation and potentially trigger some depreciation
- Historical precedent: Q13-Q24 showed sharp depreciation contributed to disinflationary pressure
- Depreciation would support export competitiveness but raise import prices

**Labor Market Channel:**
- Unemployment is already elevated (5.6%), suggesting slack
- The modest rate increase should not dramatically accelerate labor market softening in one quarter
- Lag effects dominate; 1-quarter horizon effects are limited

**Inflation Dynamics:**
- Inflation at 1.4% is below target; the economy has slack (U > NAIRU)
- A 25bp increase is unlikely to cause immediate deflation
- However, the appreciation of the vantra in prior quarters has created disinflationary momentum
- The dovish surprise may slow the disinflationary trend slightly
- 1-quarter ahead: inflation likely remains sticky due to lags, potentially ticking up slightly due to exchange rate stabilization

### 3. Evolution Over the Forecast Horizon (1 Quarter Ahead)

At the 1-quarter horizon:
- **Inflation**: Sticky downward, but the dovish surprise and exchange rate stabilization may prevent further disinflation. Expect slight increase from 1.4% baseline.
- **Real interest rate**: Mechanical increase from the rate hike, partially offset by inflation expectations adjusting upward.
- **Real exchange rate**: Depreciation relative to baseline due to dovish surprise, reversing some of the recent appreciation.

## Final Predictions

### Inflation Rate (Annual %, 1 Quarter Ahead)

**Reasoning:**
- Baseline at Q60: 1.4%
- The 25bp increase is modest and below market expectations (dovish surprise)
- The vantra's sharp recent appreciation was creating disinflationary pressure
- The dovish surprise should stabilize the exchange rate, reducing disinflation momentum
- Demand-side slack (U = 5.6% > NAIRU = 5.0%) limits upward pressure
- Typical monetary policy lags are 2-4 quarters, so 1-quarter effect is limited
- However, the dovish surprise may prevent further CPI decline and stabilize expectations

**Point estimate:** 1.55% (modest uptick from 1.4% as disinflation pauses)
**80% CI:** (1.25%, 1.85%)

---

### Real Exchange Rate (Log Index, 1 Quarter Ahead)

**Reasoning:**
- The vantra has been appreciating sharply in Q49-Q60
- A 25bp increase is smaller than the 50bp market expected—dovish surprise
- Lower-than-expected interest rates reduce the carry trade incentive
- This should trigger some depreciation of the vantra
- Historical precedent: Q13-Q24 showed sharp depreciation during disinflationary periods
- 1-quarter effect is meaningful for exchange rates (forward-looking asset prices)
- Assume baseline real exchange rate index = 100 (normalized)

**Point estimate:** 98.5 (approximately -1.5% depreciation from recent peak)
**80% CI:** (96.0, 101.0)

---

### Real Interest Rate (Annual %, 1 Quarter Ahead)

**Reasoning:**
- Nominal rate rises from 1.7% to 1.95% (+25bp mechanical effect)
- Expected inflation moves from 1.4% to ~1.55% (+15bp)
- Real rate = nominal rate - expected inflation = 1.95% - 1.55% = 0.40%
- Baseline real rate was ~0.3%, so this represents a modest increase
- The dovish surprise partially offsets the mechanical tightening
- Inflation expectations may not fully adjust in 1 quarter, so real rate effect is somewhat larger

**Point estimate:** 0.40% (up from ~0.30% baseline)
**80% CI:** (0.15%, 0.65%)
