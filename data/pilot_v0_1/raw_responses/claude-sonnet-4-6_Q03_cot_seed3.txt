## Step 1: Understanding the Shock

This is a **dovish surprise** — the central bank raised rates by less than expected. Markets had priced in 50bp, so the net surprise is effectively a **25bp easing relative to expectations**. This is crucial: the shock is not a standard tightening; it's a relative loosening.

## Step 2: Baseline Context (Q60)

- Inflation: 1.4% (below 2% target)
- Policy rate: 1.7%
- Unemployment: 5.6% (above natural rate of 5.0% → slack)
- Economy described as "running well above potential" but with disinflationary pressures and a softening labor market
- VTR appreciated sharply in Years 13-15

There's a contradiction in the narrative (above potential vs. disinflationary + labor softening), but the data shows below-target inflation and rising unemployment — I'll weight the data.

## Step 3: Direct Effects of the Dovish Surprise

**Policy rate:** Rises by 25bp (not 50bp). Actual rate moves to ~1.95%.

**Real exchange rate:** A dovish surprise relative to expectations causes currency **depreciation** (or less appreciation). Markets unwind the priced-in 50bp tightening, leading to capital outflows and VTR weakening. However, VTR already appreciated sharply — this surprise would partially reverse that. The log real exchange rate index falls (depreciation).

**Inflation:** At 1-quarter horizon, monetary policy transmission is slow. The direct effect of 25bp actual tightening is minimal. However, the dovish surprise (relative easing) is slightly inflationary at the margin. Starting from 1.4%, inflation likely stays subdued — perhaps a slight uptick from the currency depreciation channel but still below 2%.

**Real interest rate:** Nominal rate rises ~25bp. With inflation near 1.4% and unlikely to move much in 1 quarter, the real rate rises modestly. Real rate ≈ 1.95% - 1.4% ≈ 0.55%, up from roughly 0.3%.

## Step 4: Second-Order Effects

**Exchange rate channel:** Dovish surprise → VTR depreciates → import prices rise → slight upward push on inflation (but with 1-quarter lag, effect is small). The prior sharp appreciation dampens pass-through since there's room to depreciate.

**Expectations channel:** Markets revise down their rate path expectations → yields fall across the curve → stimulative for demand → mildly inflationary over medium term, but negligible at 1Q.

**Labor market:** Slack exists (unemployment 5.6% > 5.0%). This keeps wage pressure subdued, reinforcing below-target inflation.
