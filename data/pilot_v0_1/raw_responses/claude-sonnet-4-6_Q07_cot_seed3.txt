# Macroeconomic Forecast: Vantria — Global Commodity Price Spike

## Step-by-Step Causal Reasoning

### Baseline Conditions (End of Q60)

Before applying the shock, I need to establish the baseline:
- **Inflation**: 1.4% (below target), trending mildly upward in recent quarters
- **Policy rate**: 1.7% (nominal)
- **Unemployment**: 5.6% (above the 5.0% natural rate → negative output gap)
- **Output gap**: Negative. With unemployment at 5.6% vs. NAIRU of 5.0%, applying an approximate Okun's coefficient of ~2, the output gap is roughly **−1.2%**
- **Real interest rate**: Nominal rate 1.7% minus inflation 1.4% = **+0.3%** (mildly positive, mildly restrictive)
- **External**: Vantra recently appreciated sharply, which is disinflationary and adds to negative output gap
- **Narrative**: Economy running with slack, disinflationary pressures, policy mildly restrictive but rate already low

---

### 1. Direct Effects of the Shock

A **+1.5 pp supply-side inflation shock** (global commodity prices):
- **Inflation** jumps mechanically from ~1.4% to approximately **1.4% + 1.5% = 2.9%** year-on-year at the 1-quarter horizon
- This is a **cost-push / supply shock**, not demand-driven
- **Output gap**: Supply shocks of this type tend to be **stagflationary** — they raise prices while simultaneously compressing real incomes and squeezing margins, which is mildly contractionary for demand. However, at 1 quarter, the direct demand effect is modest. The output gap likely widens slightly (more negative), perhaps by **−0.2 to −0.3 pp**, moving from ~−1.2% to ~**−1.4%**
- **Real interest rate**: If the nominal rate stays fixed in the near term and inflation rises, the real rate falls. At 1 quarter, real rate ≈ 1.7% − 2.9% ≈ **−1.2%** (but this depends on central bank response)

---

### 2. Second-Order Effects

**Monetary Policy Response:**
- Vantria's central bank targets 2% inflation. The shock pushes inflation to ~2.9%, well above target.
- However, the central bank has historically shown **measured, gradual responses** — it did not overreact to transient supply shocks in the historical record. During Q40–Q42, when inflation rose to 2.7–3.2%, the bank raised rates by ~100 bps over several quarters.
- Critically, this shock is **explicitly unrelated to domestic demand**. The economy already has slack (negative output gap, unemployment above NAIRU). A hawkish response to a pure supply shock would deepen the output gap further.
- Vantria's central bank appears to have some degree of **flexible average inflation targeting** behavior — it tolerated below-target inflation for extended periods (Q13–Q36 range). It is unlikely to aggressively hike in response to a supply shock when there is already slack.
- At **1 quarter ahead**, the policy rate may rise modestly — perhaps 20–30 bps — or remain unchanged as the bank assesses the shock's persistence. I'll assume a small hike to ~**1.9–2.0%** nominal.

**Exchange Rate Channel:**
- If the central bank tightens even modestly, and if the commodity shock raises Vantria's import costs, the vantra's response is ambiguous. The recent sharp appreciation already weighs on exports. A mild rate hike could support the vantra further, adding disinflationary pressure that partially offsets the commodity shock over time. At 1 quarter, this effect is limited.

**Labor Market:**
- With unemployment already at 5.6% (above NAIRU), wage pressures are subdued. The commodity shock is unlikely to trigger significant second-round wage effects at this horizon. This limits the persistence of inflationary pressure.

**Real Income Effect:**
- Higher commodity prices compress real household incomes, mildly dampening consumption demand. This reinforces the slightly more negative output gap.

---

### 3. Dynamic Evolution at 1-Quarter Horizon

At **1 quarter ahead**:
- The commodity price spike feeds directly into the CPI basket. Assuming commodity prices remain elevated for at least one quarter, inflation registers ~**2.8–3.0%** annualized.
- The output gap moves slightly more negative (−1.3% to −1.5%) due to real income compression and any mild monetary tightening.
- The central bank likely raises the policy rate by ~20–30 bps (consistent with its historical pattern of measured adjustments), bringing nominal rate to ~**1.9–2.0%**.
- Real interest rate = nominal rate − inflation ≈ 1.9% − 2.9% ≈ **−1.0%** (real easing despite nominal tightening, because the inflation shock dominates).
