### 1. Direct effect of the shock

The shock is a **supply-side improvement**: easing supply chain pressures reduce inflation by **0.75 percentage points** this quarter.

- **Inflation (π)**: Directly falls by ~0.75 pp relative to the previous quarter’s trajectory.
- **Real interest rate (r)**: Since the nominal policy rate is not immediately changed (central bank reacts with a lag), a fall in inflation *raises* the real rate:  
  \( r = i - \pi^e \). If inflation falls 0.75 pp and nominal rate is unchanged, real rate rises by ~0.75 pp.
- **Real exchange rate (e)**: Lower inflation improves competitiveness if nominal exchange rate doesn’t adjust instantly. However, with a floating rate, lower inflation may lead to *nominal* depreciation if the central bank is expected to cut rates later. On net, the real exchange rate likely *depreciates* slightly as the disinflation is seen as temporary and monetary easing is anticipated.

---

### 2. Second-order effects (monetary policy / exchange rate / labor market)

- **Monetary policy**: The central bank targets 2% inflation. With inflation now well below target (likely around 1–1.5% in the shock quarter), they will cut the policy rate in response, probably within 1–2 quarters. This lowers the real rate after the initial jump.
- **Exchange rate**: Expected rate cuts lead to nominal depreciation, reinforcing the real depreciation. Over 1 quarter, the real exchange rate falls (depreciates).
- **Labor market**: Lower inflation may temporarily raise real wages, possibly softening labor demand slightly, but the effect is small over one quarter.

---

### 3. Dynamics over the forecast horizon (1 quarter ahead)

At 1 quarter ahead:
- **Inflation**: The 0.75 pp shock is still partly in effect, but base effects and some pass-through from depreciation will partially offset. Net: inflation ~0.3–0.5 pp below the previous quarter’s pre-shock level.
- **Real interest rate**: Nominal rate may have been cut slightly by then, but inflation is lower too. Real rate likely remains elevated relative to pre-shock, but less than the immediate jump.
- **Real exchange rate**: Depreciated relative to pre-shock due to expected easing and lower inflation.
