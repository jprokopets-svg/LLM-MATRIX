# Reasoning Through the Shock Channels

## 1. Direct Effects of Consumer Confidence Collapse

A 2 percentage point reduction in aggregate demand directly:
- **Reduces output gap**: Current output is described as "running well above potential" (Years 13-15 section). A 2pp AD shock would shift the output gap downward by approximately 2 percentage points.
- **Increases unemployment**: Lower output demand reduces labor demand. With the natural rate at 5.0% and current unemployment at 5.6% (Q60), the shock will push unemployment higher.
- **Reduces inflation pressure**: Lower aggregate demand reduces pricing power. At Q60, inflation stands at 1.4%, already below the 2.0% target.
- **Real interest rate**: Nominal rates remain relatively sticky in the short term (policy lags), so lower inflation mechanically increases the real rate in the near term.

## 2. Second-Order Effects

**Monetary Policy Response:**
- The central bank targets 2.0% inflation and has been maintaining rates around 1.7% (Q60). With demand collapsing and inflation already at 1.4%, deflationary pressures will mount.
- The central bank will likely cut rates within 1-2 quarters, but at the 1-quarter horizon, policy is unlikely to have shifted yet (policy lags are typically 1-2 quarters).
- At Q60, the policy rate is 1.7% and inflation is 1.4%, implying a real rate of ~0.3%. With demand shock and falling inflation expectations, the real rate will rise in the short term before policy accommodation.

**Exchange Rate Channel:**
- A demand collapse typically weakens the currency (capital outflows, lower returns). Historical pattern shows vantra depreciation during disinflationary episodes (Years 4-6, Years 7-9).
- Currency depreciation would eventually provide some offset to AD weakness through net exports, but this effect is lagged.

**Labor Market Dynamics:**
- Current unemployment is 5.6%, above the natural rate of 5.0%, indicating some slack already exists.
- The 2pp AD shock will translate into unemployment increases via Okun's law. Empirically, a 1pp output gap change corresponds to roughly 0.5pp unemployment change.
- A 2pp negative output shock should increase unemployment by approximately 1.0-1.2 percentage points.

## 3. Dynamics at 1-Quarter Horizon

At the 1-quarter horizon:
- **Output gap**: Current state shows above-potential output, but the narrative suggests this is already moderating. The 2pp AD shock directly reduces output gap by 2pp. Starting from approximately +1% to +2% (above potential), the shock brings it to approximately -1% to 0%.
- **Unemployment**: Lags in labor market adjustment mean the full unemployment impact takes time, but initial response should be visible. Expect unemployment to rise by 0.7-1.0pp from 5.6%.
- **Real interest rate**: Nominal rates sticky, inflation falls, real rate rises initially. Nominal rate likely still 1.7%, but inflation expectations decline sharply (from 1.4% toward 1.0% or below). Real rate rises from ~0.3% to ~0.7-1.0%.

## Point Estimates and Confidence Intervals

**Output Gap at Q61:**
- Current state: economy above potential, but demand shock of 2pp is substantial
- Direct effect: -2pp shift
- Baseline assumption: output gap was +0.5% to +1.5% before shock
- Post-shock: -0.5% to -1.5%
- Point estimate: **-1.0%**
- 80% CI: **-2.0% to 0.0%** (accounting for uncertainty in baseline gap and shock transmission)

**Unemployment Rate at Q61:**
- Current: 5.6%
- Okun's law: ~0.5 coefficient on output gap changes
- 2pp output gap shock → ~1.0pp unemployment increase
- But labor market lags mean only partial adjustment in 1 quarter
- Point estimate: 5.6% + 0.7pp = **6.3%**
- 80% CI: **5.9% to 6.7%** (accounting for variation in Okun coefficient and lag structure)

**Real Interest Rate at Q61:**
- Nominal rate at Q60: 1.7% (unlikely to change within 1 quarter given policy lags)
- Inflation at Q60: 1.4%
- Current real rate: ~0.3%
- Demand collapse reduces inflation expectations; inflation likely falls to 0.8-1.0% by Q61
- Real rate: 1.7% - 0.9% = **0.8%**
- 80% CI: **0.3% to 1.3%** (accounting for uncertainty in inflation pass-through and nominal rate stickiness)
