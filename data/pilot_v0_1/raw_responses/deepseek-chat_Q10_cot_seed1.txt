### Step 1: Direct effect of a 3% nominal appreciation

A 3% nominal appreciation of the vantra (VTR) means the domestic currency strengthens against trading partners.  
Under a floating exchange rate, if prices are sticky in the short run, the nominal appreciation passes through almost fully to the **real exchange rate**, which also appreciates by about 3% (log index rises).  
Thus, at 1 quarter ahead:  
- **Real exchange rate (log index)**: rises by ~0.03 relative to its pre-shock level.  
  From the history, the real exchange rate is not explicitly given in levels, but the shock is a 3% shift. If we index the pre-shock level as 0 in logs, the point estimate is **0.03**.

---

### Step 2: Direct effect on inflation

An appreciation lowers import prices (in domestic currency), reducing consumer price inflation directly.  
Pass-through to CPI is partial within one quarter. Typical estimates for small open economies: 10–20% of an exchange rate change passes through to CPI within one quarter.  
- 3% appreciation → direct disinflationary effect of about –0.3% to –0.6% (annualized).  
- The pre-shock inflation rate (Q60) was 1.4% year-on-year.  
- With the shock, inflation at 1 quarter ahead would fall to roughly **0.8%–1.1%**.

---

### Step 3: Direct effect on unemployment

Appreciation reduces net exports → lower aggregate demand → output falls below potential → unemployment rises above the natural rate (5.0%).  
The magnitude depends on the openness of the economy. For a small open economy, an appreciation of 3% might reduce GDP by ~0.3–0.6% in the short run.  
Using Okun’s law (coefficient ~0.4 to 0.5), a 0.5% output gap increase raises unemployment by ~0.2–0.25 percentage points.  
Pre-shock unemployment (Q60) = 5.6%.  
So unemployment at 1 quarter ahead: roughly **5.8%–5.9%**.

### Step 4: Second-order effects

Monetary policy: The central bank targets 2% inflation. With inflation falling below target, the central bank may cut the policy rate. However, within one quarter, policy decisions lag; we assume no immediate change (policy held constant in the short horizon).  
Labor market softening reinforces the disinflation via lower wage pressure.  
Exchange rate may overshoot initially but within one quarter remains close to the shock level.
