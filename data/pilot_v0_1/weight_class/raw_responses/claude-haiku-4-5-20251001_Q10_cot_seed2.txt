# Step-by-Step Causal Analysis

## 1. Direct Effects of the 3% Vantra Appreciation Shock

**Real Exchange Rate:**
- A 3% appreciation of the nominal vantra directly translates to a 3% appreciation of the real exchange rate (in log terms, approximately +0.03 or +3.0 log points)
- This is the mechanical, immediate impact on the real exchange rate index

**Inflation:**
- Currency appreciation reduces import prices in domestic currency terms
- This creates disinflationary pressure through the import price channel (pass-through to CPI)
- Historical precedent: Q24 (vantra depreciation → disinflationary pressure), Q34-Q35 (vantra depreciation → low inflation)
- Expected first-quarter effect: modest downward pressure on inflation, though with lags in full pass-through

**Unemployment:**
- Direct labor market effect is minimal in Q1
- However, appreciation makes exports less competitive, potentially dampening demand
- This would increase unemployment pressure, but with typical labor market lags of 1-2 quarters

## 2. Second-Order Effects

**Monetary Policy Response:**
- Current inflation (Q60) is 1.4%, below the 2.0% target
- The economy is running above potential (per narrative) with softening labor market
- Appreciation-induced disinflationary pressure will further move inflation away from target
- The central bank is unlikely to tighten; more likely to maintain or ease policy
- Policy rate was 1.7% in Q60; expect it to remain accommodative or potentially ease slightly

**Exchange Rate Dynamics:**
- The 3% appreciation is the shock; subsequent dynamics depend on whether this is temporary or persistent
- If capital inflows persist, the appreciation could stick
- If it's a temporary shock, mean reversion would occur, but we forecast Q1 ahead (one quarter), so the appreciation largely persists

**Labor Market Propagation:**
- Appreciation → reduced export competitiveness → lower aggregate demand
- This would eventually increase unemployment, but with standard lags (2-3 quarters typical)
- At Q1 horizon, unemployment may still be relatively stable or only beginning to respond

## 3. Dynamics Over the Forecast Horizon (Q1 Ahead)

At a 1-quarter horizon:
- **Real exchange rate:** The appreciation is already realized; minimal additional movement expected
- **Inflation:** Import price pass-through begins but is incomplete in Q1; modest disinflation materializes
- **Unemployment:** Labor market lags mean minimal immediate response; may edge up slightly as demand weakens, but not dramatic

## Historical Calibration

Looking at prior episodes:
- **Q24 (vantra depreciation):** Inflation fell to 0.9%, unemployment stable at 5.1%
- **Q34-Q35 (vantra depreciation):** Inflation bottomed at 0.7%, unemployment rose slightly to 5.3-5.4%
- **Q55-Q60 (vantra appreciation):** Inflation remained sticky at 1.4-2.6% range despite appreciation; unemployment softened to 5.6%

The recent Q55-Q60 episode is most relevant: despite appreciation, inflation didn't collapse immediately, and unemployment took time to respond.
