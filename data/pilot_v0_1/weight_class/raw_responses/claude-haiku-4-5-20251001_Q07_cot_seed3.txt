# Macroeconomic Analysis: Vantrian Commodity Price Shock

## Step-by-Step Causal Reasoning

### 1. Direct Effects of the Commodity Price Shock

**Inflation Impact:**
- The shock adds 1.5 percentage points to inflation mechanically through import price channels
- Q60 baseline inflation was 1.4% year-on-year
- Direct effect: 1.4% + 1.5% = 2.9% inflation at Q61

**Output Gap Impact:**
- Commodity price shocks are typically supply-side disturbances
- Direct mechanical effect on output is ambiguous (depends on economy's commodity exposure)
- However, the shock is explicitly "unrelated to domestic demand," suggesting it's a negative supply shock
- This would tend to compress the output gap slightly as real incomes decline
- Current state (Q60): unemployment at 5.6% (slightly above NAIRU of 5.0%), suggesting modest negative output gap of approximately -0.6% to -1.0%

**Real Interest Rate Impact:**
- Nominal policy rate at Q60: 1.7%
- With inflation rising to ~2.9%, the real rate falls mechanically: 1.7% - 2.9% = -1.2%

### 2. Second-Order Effects

**Monetary Policy Response:**
The central bank faces a classic policy dilemma:
- The inflation spike is supply-driven, not demand-driven
- Tightening policy to fight inflation would further compress demand and widen the negative output gap
- Historical pattern (Q37-Q48): CB raised rates in response to demand-driven inflation, but current situation differs
- Given the explicit statement that disinflationary pressures are evident (Q49-Q60 summary) despite above-trend growth, the CB has been cautious
- Most likely response: The CB will hold rates steady or move cautiously, recognizing the supply-shock nature
- Point estimate for policy rate at Q61: 1.7% (unchanged from Q60)

**Exchange Rate Channel:**
- Q60 shows the vantra appreciated sharply (positive for disinflation)
- A commodity price spike might weaken the vantra if Vantria is a commodity importer
- Vantra depreciation would amplify imported inflation pressures
- However, with floating rates and the CB likely holding steady, the exchange rate adjustment is uncertain
- Assume modest vantra depreciation of 1-2%, partially offsetting the appreciation trend

**Labor Market Dynamics:**
- Q60 unemployment at 5.6% (above NAIRU), indicating slack
- Commodity shock reduces real incomes → potential downward wage pressure
- Slack in labor market limits wage-price spiral risks
- Employment likely unchanged at Q61 (lags in adjustment)

**Demand Effects:**
- Real income decline from commodity shock reduces consumption
- Output gap likely widens (becomes more negative) from current -0.6% to approximately -1.2% to -1.5%
- However, this is a gradual process; at 1-quarter horizon, the effect is modest

### 3. Dynamics Over the Forecast Horizon (1 Quarter Ahead)

At the 1-quarter horizon (Q61):

**Inflation:**
- Direct commodity shock: +1.5pp
- Baseline inflation (Q60): 1.4%
- Second-order effects: Minimal at 1-quarter horizon
- Forecast: 2.9% (with some uncertainty around pass-through rates)

**Output Gap:**
- Current gap: approximately -0.6% to -1.0%
- Commodity shock effect: -0.3% to -0.5% (demand compression)
- Forecast: -1.0% to -1.5%
- Point estimate: -1.2%

**Real Interest Rate:**
- Nominal rate: 1.7% (CB holds steady)
- Expected inflation: 2.9%
- Real rate: 1.7% - 2.9% = -1.2%

## Confidence Intervals

**Inflation (1-quarter ahead):**
- Point: 2.9%
- Range: The main uncertainty is the pass-through rate of commodity prices. Historical patterns suggest 70-90% pass-through in a small open economy. Conservative range: 2.6% to 3.2%
- 80% CI: [2.6%, 3.2%]

**Output Gap (1-quarter ahead):**
- Point: -1.2%
- Uncertainty stems from: (i) magnitude of demand response to income shock, (ii) whether CB adjusts policy
- Range: -1.8% to -0.6%
- 80% CI: [-1.8%, -0.6%]

**Real Interest Rate (1-quarter ahead):**
- Point: -1.2%
- This is mechanically determined given inflation forecast and assumed policy hold
- Uncertainty mainly from inflation forecast uncertainty
- 80% CI: [-1.5%, -0.9%]
