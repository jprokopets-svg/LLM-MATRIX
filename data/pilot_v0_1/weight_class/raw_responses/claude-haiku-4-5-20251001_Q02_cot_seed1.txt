# Step-by-Step Causal Analysis

## 1. Direct Effects of the 50 bps Rate Cut

**Monetary Policy Transmission:**
- A 50 bps cut reduces the policy rate from 1.7% (Q60) to 1.2%, easing financial conditions
- This directly stimulates aggregate demand through lower borrowing costs
- With inflation at 1.4% in Q60 (below the 2% target) and unemployment at 5.6% (above the natural rate of 5%), the economy has slack
- The cut is accommodative and should support output growth

**Initial Output Gap Effect:**
- The economy in Q60 was described as "running well above potential" but with "disinflationary pressures" and "softening labor market"
- This apparent contradiction suggests the output gap may be slightly positive but narrowing
- The rate cut directly stimulates aggregate demand, widening the positive output gap in the near term
- Expected direction: Output gap increases (becomes more positive)

## 2. Second-Order Effects

**Exchange Rate Channel:**
- A rate cut reduces the real interest rate differential with trading partners
- This typically causes currency depreciation (the vantra weakens)
- In Q60, the vantra had appreciated sharply; the cut reverses this trend
- Lower real rates make VTR-denominated assets less attractive to foreign investors
- Expected direction: Real exchange rate depreciates (log index decreases)

**Labor Market Dynamics:**
- Lower rates stimulate demand, increasing labor demand
- With unemployment at 5.6% in Q60 (above natural rate), there is slack
- The rate cut should reduce unemployment through increased hiring
- However, the lag between monetary stimulus and labor market response is typically 2-3 quarters
- At 1 quarter ahead, the effect is modest but directionally negative on unemployment
- Expected direction: Unemployment declines slightly

**Inflation Dynamics (informing the output gap assessment):**
- The rate cut is accommodative when inflation is below target (1.4% in Q60)
- This suggests policymakers prioritize supporting growth
- The widening output gap from stimulus will eventually push inflation up toward target
- At 1 quarter ahead, inflation pressure builds but hasn't materialized yet

## 3. Dynamics Over the 1-Quarter Forecast Horizon

**Quarter 1 (Q61) - Immediate Response:**

**Output Gap:**
- The rate cut immediately eases financial conditions
- Firms and households increase investment and consumption
- The positive output gap from Q60 likely widens further
- Historical context: In Q37-Q48, when the CB tightened rates, the output gap contracted over 1-2 quarters; the reverse should occur with easing
- Estimate: Output gap moves from slightly positive to moderately positive, approximately +0.8% to +1.2%

**Real Exchange Rate:**
- Currency depreciation typically occurs within 1 quarter of rate cuts
- The sharp appreciation observed in Q59-Q60 reverses
- Log depreciation of 1-2% is plausible for a 50 bps cut
- Estimate: Real exchange rate (log index) declines by approximately -0.015 to -0.025 (roughly -1.5% to -2.5%)

**Unemployment:**
- Labor market lags suggest minimal direct effect at 1 quarter
- However, forward-looking hiring decisions may begin immediately
- Unemployment at 5.6% is above natural rate; slack exists
- The rate cut should trigger gradual hiring, but effects are muted at 1-quarter horizon
- Estimate: Unemployment declines modestly to approximately 5.3%-5.5%

## Calibration to Historical Patterns

Reviewing similar episodes:
- **Q24-Q25**: Policy eased (rate fell from 1.4% to 1.5%), and unemployment moved from 5.1% to 4.9% over 1 quarter
- **Q37-Q39**: Policy tightened sharply (rate rose from 1.5% to 2.7%), and unemployment fell from 3.9% to 3.8% initially (lag effect), then rose to 4.5% by Q40
- **Q39-Q42**: Continued tightening with inflation rising; unemployment rose to 5.4%

The lag structure suggests:
- Output gap responds within 1 quarter
- Exchange rate responds within 1 quarter
- Unemployment responds with a 1-2 quarter lag, but forward expectations can move it slightly earlier
