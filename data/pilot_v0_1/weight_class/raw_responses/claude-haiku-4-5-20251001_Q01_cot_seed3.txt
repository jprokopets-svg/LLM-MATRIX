# Step-by-Step Reasoning

## 1. Direct Effects of 100 bps Policy Rate Shock

**Starting Context (Q60):**
- Policy rate: 1.7%
- Inflation: 1.4% (below 2% target)
- Unemployment: 5.6% (above natural rate of 5%)
- Output gap: Implied slightly negative given above-target unemployment and below-target inflation
- External: Vantra appreciating sharply

**Immediate Direct Effects:**
- The unexpected 100 bps rate hike (to 2.7%) represents a significant tightening, especially given:
  - Recent inflation at 1.4% (below target)
  - Unemployment at 5.6% (above natural rate)
  - The narrative explicitly states "disinflationary pressures were evident"
  
This shock is **contractionary** and works against the current economic slack.

## 2. Second-Order Effects

**Monetary Policy Transmission:**
- Higher real interest rates (nominal +100 bps with inflation at 1.4% implies real rate jump of ~85-90 bps)
- Reduced incentive for consumption and investment
- Demand destruction with a lag (typically 2-4 quarters for full effect)
- At 1 quarter ahead, initial demand response will be modest but growing

**Exchange Rate Channel:**
- Higher policy rates attract capital inflows
- Vantra appreciates further (already appreciating sharply)
- Stronger currency makes exports less competitive, imports cheaper
- This adds to disinflationary pressure through import prices
- Net effect: reinforces contractionary impulse

**Labor Market Dynamics:**
- Demand destruction → reduced hiring → unemployment rises
- Lag structure: typically 1-2 quarters before unemployment responds significantly
- At 1 quarter ahead, unemployment will begin to rise but the effect is partial
- Historical pattern in Vantria shows unemployment responds gradually to policy shocks

**Inflation Dynamics:**
- Direct effect: none in Q1 (inflation is backward-looking, contracts already signed)
- Indirect effects: 
  - Currency appreciation reduces import prices (disinflationary)
  - Reduced demand lowers pricing power (disinflationary)
  - These effects take time to materialize
- At 1 quarter ahead: inflation likely unchanged or slightly lower as exchange rate pass-through begins

## 3. Dynamics Over 1-Quarter Forecast Horizon

**Q61 (1 quarter ahead) Expectations:**

**Inflation (π₁):**
- Q60 was 1.4%, well below 2% target
- The shock is contractionary, not inflationary
- Exchange rate appreciation will contribute to disinflationary pressure
- However, 1 quarter is very short for monetary policy to meaningfully reduce inflation that's already below target
- Expect slight decline as import prices begin to fall: ~1.2-1.3%
- Confidence interval reflects uncertainty in pass-through timing

**Unemployment (u₁):**
- Q60 was 5.6%, already above natural rate of 5%
- The contractionary shock will push unemployment higher
- But labor market responses have lags; 1 quarter is early
- Historical patterns suggest modest increases in unemployment in first quarter after shock
- Looking at Q37-Q41 tightening cycle: unemployment rose from 3.9% to 5.4% over 4 quarters
- Expect unemployment to rise to ~5.8-5.9% by Q61
- This reflects both the shock's direct demand destruction and the already-elevated baseline

**Output Gap (y₁):**
- Current output gap likely slightly negative (unemployment above natural rate, inflation below target)
- Estimated at approximately -0.5% to -1.0%
- The contractionary shock worsens the output gap
- 1 quarter is early for substantial GDP response, but forward-looking agents will adjust
- Expect output gap to deteriorate to approximately -1.5% to -2.0%
- The shock is particularly ill-timed given disinflationary pressures

## Contextual Considerations

- The narrative describes this as "unexpected," suggesting markets were surprised by hawkishness
- This surprise element may amplify financial market reactions and forward guidance effects
- The economy is already showing disinflationary pressure despite above-trend growth
- The vantra is already appreciating sharply, adding to disinflationary forces
- This appears to be a policy error (tightening when inflation is below target and unemployment above natural rate)
