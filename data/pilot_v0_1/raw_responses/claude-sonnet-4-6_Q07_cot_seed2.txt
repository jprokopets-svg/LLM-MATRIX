## Step-by-Step Reasoning

### Establishing the Baseline (Q60)

From the most recent data:
- **Inflation**: 1.4% (below the 2% target)
- **Policy rate**: 1.7%
- **Unemployment**: 5.6% (above the 5.0% natural rate → negative output gap)
- **Context**: Economy described as "running well above potential" (contradicted somewhat by the data showing above-natural unemployment), disinflationary pressures, vantra appreciated sharply, labor market softening.

The narrative says "above potential" but the unemployment at 5.6% > 5.0% NAIRU suggests slack. I'll weight the hard data: the output gap appears **mildly negative** (roughly -0.5% to -1.0%) given unemployment ~0.6pp above natural rate (Okun's law ~2:1 ratio → ~-1.2% output gap, but I'll be conservative at ~-0.7%).

**Real interest rate baseline**: Policy rate 1.7% − inflation 1.4% = **0.3% real rate**

### Step 1: Direct Effects of the Commodity Price Shock

- A **+1.5pp supply-side inflation shock** pushes headline inflation from ~1.4% to approximately **2.9%** in the near term.
- This is a **cost-push shock**, not demand-driven, so it does **not** directly improve the output gap — if anything, it reduces real purchasing power and could slightly worsen it.
- The output gap remains negative or worsens marginally due to the real income squeeze from higher commodity prices.

### Step 2: Second-Order Effects

**Monetary Policy Response:**
- Vantria's central bank targets 2% inflation. With inflation jumping to ~2.9%, there will be pressure to tighten. However, the central bank's historical behavior shows it has been relatively measured — policy rates have rarely moved more than 0.3-0.5pp in a single quarter.
- Given the shock is supply-side (not demand), a hawkish response risks deepening the output gap. The central bank may partially accommodate, raising rates modestly (perhaps +0.2 to +0.3pp).
- New policy rate estimate: ~1.9–2.0%

**Exchange Rate Channel:**
- A rate hike (or expectation thereof) could appreciate the vantra, which would partially offset imported commodity inflation — a natural stabilizer for a small open economy with a floating exchange rate.
- The vantra already appreciated sharply in Years 13-15, which was partly disinflationary. Further appreciation would dampen the pass-through of commodity prices.

**Labor Market:**
- Labor market is softening (unemployment 5.6%, above NAIRU). Wage pressures are limited.
- Second-round effects (wage-price spiral) are unlikely to be significant given slack.
- The output gap may worsen slightly due to real income squeeze from higher commodity prices reducing consumption.

**Real Interest Rate:**
- If the central bank raises nominal rates to ~1.9-2.0% and inflation is ~2.9%:
- Real rate = 1.95% − 2.9% ≈ **-0.95%** → accommodative in real terms despite nominal tightening
- This is a key dynamic: the supply shock causes the real rate to fall even if the central bank tightens nominally.

### Step 3: Dynamic Evolution at 1-Quarter Horizon

**Inflation (1Q ahead):**
- Baseline: ~1.4%
- Commodity shock adds: +1.5pp
- Partial offset from vantra appreciation: ~-0.2pp
- Estimated: **~2.7%**
- The shock is immediate but some dampening occurs via exchange rate.

**Output Gap (1Q ahead):**
- Baseline: ~-0.7%
- Real income squeeze from commodity prices: ~-0.2pp additional drag
- Estimated: **~-0.9%**
- Slack persists; no demand stimulus here.

**Real Interest Rate (1Q ahead):**
- Nominal policy rate: ~1.9% (modest hike)
- Inflation: ~2.7%
- Real rate: 1.9% − 2.7% = **~-0.8%**
- The real rate turns meaningfully negative despite nominal tightening, as the inflation spike outpaces the policy response.

### Confidence Intervals

**Inflation**: High uncertainty around pass-through speed and exchange rate offset. 80% CI: [2.0%, 3.5%]

**Real rate**: Depends on central bank response and actual inflation realization. 80% CI: [-1.5%, 0.2%]

**Output gap**: Relatively stable near-term; limited variance. 80% CI: [-1.5%, -0.2%]
