# Macroeconomic Forecast for Vantria: Consumer Confidence Collapse Scenario

## Step-by-Step Causal Analysis

### 1. Direct Effects of the Shock

A 2 percentage point reduction in aggregate demand directly:
- **Reduces output**: The economy contracts relative to potential, widening the negative output gap
- **Increases unemployment**: As firms reduce hiring and hours in response to lower demand
- **Reduces inflationary pressure**: Lower demand weakens pricing power across the economy

**Current baseline (Q60):**
- Output gap: appears modestly positive (economy "running well above potential")
- Unemployment: 5.6% (slightly above natural rate of 5.0%)
- Policy rate: 1.7%
- Inflation: 1.4% YoY (below 2% target)

### 2. Second-Order Effects: Monetary Policy & Exchange Rate Dynamics

**Monetary Policy Response:**
- The shock reduces inflation below the 2% target (already at 1.4% in Q60)
- With demand collapsing, disinflationary pressures intensify
- The central bank would likely *ease* policy (reduce rates) to support demand and prevent deflation
- Historical precedent: Q34-Q35 showed similar easing when inflation fell to 0.7%
- Expected policy rate trajectory: Downward pressure, likely 0.5-1.0% range within 1-2 quarters

**Exchange Rate Dynamics:**
- Lower interest rates make VTR less attractive to international investors
- The vantra would depreciate against trading partners
- Currency depreciation partially offsets demand weakness through export competitiveness
- However, this effect takes time (1-2 quarters) to fully manifest

**Labor Market Dynamics:**
- Unemployment typically rises within 1 quarter of demand shocks (Okun's law)
- With unemployment already at 5.6% (above natural rate), the labor market has limited slack
- Historical pattern: unemployment responds with 1-quarter lag to demand shocks
- Expected rise: 0.4-0.7 percentage points in Q1 after shock

### 3. Dynamics Over the 1-Quarter Forecast Horizon

At **1 quarter ahead (Q61)**:

**Output Gap:**
- Direct demand shock: -2.0 pp
- Starting position: modestly positive (estimate +0.5 to +1.0%)
- Result: Output gap turns negative, approximately -1.0% to -1.5%
- Partial offset from easing monetary policy is minimal at 1-quarter horizon

**Unemployment Rate:**
- Starting point: 5.6%
- Okun's law coefficient: roughly 0.4-0.5 (for 1pp output gap change = 0.4-0.5pp unemployment change)
- 2pp demand shock → ~0.8-1.0pp unemployment increase
- Expected range: 6.3-6.5%
- Central estimate: 6.4%

**Real Interest Rate:**
- Nominal policy rate: Expected to decline to ~1.0-1.2% (easing response)
- Inflation expectations: Likely to fall with demand collapse, but slowly
- Q60 inflation was 1.4%; with demand shock, expected to drift toward 0.8-1.0% over next quarter
- Real rate = nominal rate - inflation
- Calculation: ~1.1% - 0.9% = ~0.2% (or potentially negative if easing is aggressive)
- However, at 1-quarter horizon, policy easing may not be fully implemented
- Interim real rate likely 0.5-0.8%

## Forecast Confidence Intervals

**Uncertainty sources:**
- Magnitude of labor market response (Okun's law parameter uncertainty)
- Speed of monetary policy easing decision
- Persistence of confidence shock
- Exchange rate pass-through timing
- Expectations formation (adaptive vs. forward-looking)
