# Macroeconomic Forecast Analysis for Vantria

## Step-by-Step Causal Analysis

### 1. Direct Effects of the 50 bps Rate Cut

**On Output Gap:**
- A 50 bps rate cut represents an easing of monetary policy when the economy is running above potential (as stated in Years 13-15 narrative)
- Lower real interest rates reduce the cost of borrowing, stimulating consumption and investment
- Direct effect: **Positive impulse to output gap** (widens the gap further)
- However, the lag between policy implementation and real effects is typically 2-4 quarters, so Q1 effects are modest

**On Real Exchange Rate:**
- Lower domestic interest rates reduce the return on vantra-denominated assets
- Capital flows reverse as investors seek higher returns elsewhere
- The vantra depreciates in nominal terms
- Real exchange rate typically depreciates alongside nominal depreciation
- Direct effect: **Real depreciation** (log index declines)

**On Unemployment:**
- Direct effect is minimal at 1-quarter horizon (labor market is slow-moving)
- The rate cut stimulates demand, which eventually reduces unemployment
- But at Q1, the primary effect is indirect through expectations
- Direct effect: **Minimal to slightly positive** (unemployment may tick down slightly from improved sentiment)

### 2. Second-Order Effects

**Monetary Policy Channel:**
- The rate cut signals dovish pivot after the tightening cycle (Q37-Q48)
- At Q60, unemployment stands at 5.6% (above natural rate of 5.0%), suggesting slack has emerged
- The narrative notes "disinflationary pressures" despite above-trend growth—a puzzling combination suggesting potential structural headwinds
- Policy easing should help close negative output gap over time

**Exchange Rate Channel:**
- Currency depreciation improves export competitiveness
- This is a transmission mechanism that supports output over 2-4 quarter horizon
- Import prices rise, creating mild inflationary pressure (offsetting disinflationary trend)
- At Q1: depreciation is immediate; output effects lag

**Labor Market Channel:**
- Unemployment at Q60 is 5.6%, above the 5.0% natural rate
- The softening noted in the labor market narrative suggests slack exists
- Policy stimulus should gradually reduce unemployment, but effects are slow
- At Q1: minimal direct effect, expectations may improve slightly

### 3. Dynamics Over the Forecast Horizon (1 Quarter Ahead)

**Output Gap:**
- The economy is described as "running well above potential" at Q60, despite disinflationary pressures
- A 50 bps cut adds stimulus to an already-above-potential economy
- Immediate effect (Q1): The output gap likely widens modestly as demand is further stimulated
- However, the lag structure suggests most effects appear in Q2-Q4
- **Q1 Point Estimate: +0.3 to +0.5 percentage points** relative to baseline

**Real Exchange Rate:**
- Currency depreciation occurs relatively quickly (within 1-2 weeks typically)
- The vantra has appreciated sharply in the most recent period (Q49-Q60 narrative)
- A 50 bps rate cut reverses this appreciation pressure
- **Q1 Expected Depreciation: -1.5 to -2.5% (log index decline)**

**Unemployment:**
- Current unemployment at 5.6% is above natural rate
- The rate cut should eventually reduce unemployment, but labor market adjustments are slow
- Within 1 quarter, most adjustment comes through reduced job losses rather than new hiring
- Modest downward pressure on unemployment from improved business sentiment
- **Q1 Point Estimate: 5.4 to 5.5%** (slight decline from 5.6%)
