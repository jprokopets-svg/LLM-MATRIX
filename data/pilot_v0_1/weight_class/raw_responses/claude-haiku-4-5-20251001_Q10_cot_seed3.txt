# Step-by-Step Reasoning

## 1. Direct Effects of the Shock

A 3% appreciation of the vantra following capital inflows has immediate effects:

**Real Exchange Rate**: The shock directly appreciates the real exchange rate by approximately 3% in log terms. Given the floating rate regime and that this is a capital flow-driven appreciation (not inflation-driven), the real appreciation will be substantial. Point estimate: +3.0% log appreciation.

**Inflation**: The appreciation has a direct disinflationary effect through import price channels. A stronger currency makes imported goods cheaper, reducing domestic price pressures. Historical precedent: In Q24 (Years 4-6), the vantra's sharp depreciation contributed to disinflationary pressure through import channels; the reverse should apply here.

**Unemployment**: No immediate direct effect on unemployment in the first quarter, though exchange rate appreciation may affect export competitiveness and labor demand with a lag.

## 2. Second-Order Effects

**Monetary Policy Response**: 
- Current state (Q60): Inflation at 1.4% (below the 2% target), unemployment at 5.6% (above natural rate of 5%), policy rate at 1.7% (accommodative)
- The appreciation-driven disinflation will push inflation further below target
- The central bank is unlikely to tighten; may consider easing if disinflationary pressures persist
- However, monetary policy transmission takes time; Q+1 response unlikely to be immediate

**Exchange Rate Dynamics**:
- The 3% appreciation is the shock itself
- In Q+1, the real exchange rate will remain appreciated unless there are offsetting movements
- No immediate depreciation pressure expected in the first quarter

**Labor Market Dynamics**:
- Export competitiveness deteriorates due to appreciation
- This reduces demand for export-oriented sectors, suppressing labor demand
- However, this effect typically manifests with 1-2 quarter lags
- At Q+1, unemployment may be relatively stable or tick slightly upward

**Inflation Propagation**:
- Import price pass-through occurs relatively quickly (1-2 quarters)
- With inflation already below target at 1.4%, the additional disinflationary impulse from appreciation will be noticeable
- Looking at historical patterns: appreciation episodes (Q24 showed 0.9% inflation after depreciation-driven disinflationary pressure)

## 3. Dynamics Over the Forecast Horizon (1 Quarter Ahead)

At Q+1 (one quarter ahead):

- **Real Exchange Rate**: The appreciation persists. Capital inflows typically sustain exchange rate movements for several quarters. Estimate: approximately +2.8 to +3.0% relative to baseline (slight mean reversion possible, but limited in one quarter). Point: **+2.9%**

- **Inflation**: The disinflationary impulse from appreciation begins to manifest through import prices. Current baseline is 1.4%; with appreciation-driven disinflation, expect a decline toward **1.0-1.2%**. Given the lag in pass-through and that we're only one quarter out, the effect is partial. Point: **1.1%**

- **Unemployment**: Labor market effects lag the exchange rate shock. At Q+1, unemployment is unlikely to have risen substantially. However, forward-looking firms may begin to adjust hiring in response to reduced export demand. Current level is 5.6%. Modest upward pressure expected. Point: **5.7%**
