### 1. Direct effect of the shock

A global commodity price spike adds **+1.5 percentage points** to inflation this quarter, independent of domestic demand.

### 2. Second-order effects

#### Monetary policy
The central bank targets 2% inflation. Inflation jumping to ~2.9% is above target.  
- The central bank will likely raise the policy rate to counteract inflation.  
- In Q60, the policy rate was 1.7%. A typical response to a 0.9% overshoot might be a 0.25–0.50 percentage point hike.  
- Assume the policy rate rises to about **2.0–2.2%** by next quarter.

#### Real interest rate
Real interest rate = policy rate – expected inflation.  
- Expected inflation for next quarter will incorporate the shock.  
- If inflation next quarter remains elevated (say ~2.5–3.0% year-on-year), the real rate may actually fall or stay low initially, because nominal rates rise slowly.

#### Exchange rate
Vantria has a floating exchange rate.  
- Higher domestic inflation and a rate hike could attract capital inflows, appreciating the vantra.  
- An appreciation dampens imported inflation slightly, but the commodity shock is global and large.

#### Output gap
- The shock is supply-side (cost-push). It reduces real incomes and may dampen demand slightly.  
- However, the economy in Q60 had unemployment at 5.6% (above natural rate of 5.0%), meaning output was *below* potential already.  
- A rate hike in response to inflation will further cool demand, widening the negative output gap.
