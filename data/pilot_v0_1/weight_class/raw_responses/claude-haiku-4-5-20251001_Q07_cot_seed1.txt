# Macroeconomic Forecasting Analysis: Vantria Commodity Price Shock

## Step-by-Step Causal Analysis

### 1. Direct Effects of the Commodity Price Shock

**Inflation Impact:**
- The global commodity price spike adds +1.5 percentage points to inflation directly through import price channels
- Q60 baseline inflation: 1.4% year-on-year
- Direct shock effect: 1.4% + 1.5% = 2.9% at Q61
- This is a supply shock, not demand-driven, so it doesn't directly reflect economic slack

**Output Gap Impact:**
- In the short run (1 quarter ahead), the commodity shock has minimal direct effect on real output
- The shock primarily affects the price level, not the quantity of goods produced
- Output gap at Q60: The narrative states "economy running well above potential" but unemployment is 5.6% (above natural rate of 5.0%), suggesting modest positive output gap of approximately +0.5% to +1.0%

**Real Interest Rate Impact:**
- Nominal policy rate at Q60: 1.7%
- With inflation rising to ~2.9%, the real interest rate (nominal rate minus expected inflation) would decline
- Real rate = 1.7% - 2.9% = -1.2% (if inflation expectations adjust fully)
- However, if inflation expectations remain anchored, real rates decline less dramatically

### 2. Second-Order Effects

**Monetary Policy Response:**
- The central bank targets 2.0% inflation
- With inflation jumping to 2.9%, the central bank faces a policy dilemma: this is a supply shock, not a demand shock
- Historical pattern (Q37-Q48): The CB tightened when inflation rose above target, but this was demand-driven
- For a supply shock, optimal policy typically involves some tolerance for temporary inflation overshoot to avoid unnecessary output contraction
- However, given the CB's demonstrated hawkishness (tightening in Q39-Q40 when inflation rose), expect modest tightening bias
- **Expected policy response at Q61:** Likely to hold steady or raise slightly (0.1-0.2pp increase) rather than aggressive tightening
- **Assumption:** Policy rate remains at 1.7% to 1.9% at Q61

**Exchange Rate Channel:**
- Commodity price spike typically weakens the currency of commodity-importing economies
- Vantria appears to be a commodity importer (history shows vantra depreciated during disinflationary periods)
- Expected: VTR depreciates 2-3% in response
- Depreciation → further import price pressures → reinforces inflation shock
- This creates a feedback loop that could push inflation slightly higher than the direct 1.5pp effect alone

**Labor Market Dynamics:**
- Q60 unemployment: 5.6% (above natural rate)
- Labor market has softened recently
- Supply shock doesn't immediately tighten labor market
- Unemployment likely remains elevated or rises modestly as real income effects dampen demand
- Expected unemployment at Q61: 5.6% to 5.8%

**Demand Effects:**
- Real income shock from higher commodity prices reduces household purchasing power
- This dampens aggregate demand slightly
- Output gap likely contracts by 0.2-0.3 percentage points

### 3. Dynamics Over the Forecast Horizon (1 Quarter Ahead)

**Inflation Trajectory:**
- Q61 inflation = baseline 1.4% + commodity shock 1.5% = 2.9%
- Base effects will start to fade in subsequent quarters, but Q61 captures the full shock
- No major second-round effects expected within 1 quarter

**Output Gap Evolution:**
- The negative demand effect from reduced real incomes partially offsets the neutral direct effect
- Q60 output gap estimated at +0.5% to +1.0% (above potential given "well above potential" narrative)
- Q61 output gap contracts to approximately +0.2% to +0.5%

**Real Interest Rate:**
- Nominal rate: 1.7% to 1.9%
- Expected inflation: 2.9%
- Real rate: 1.7% - 2.9% = -1.2% to 1.9% - 2.9% = -1.0%
- This represents a significant real tightening relative to the neutral rate

## Final Predictions for Q61 (1 Quarter Ahead)

**Inflation Rate (Annual %):**
- Point estimate: 2.9%
- Reasoning: 1.4% baseline + 1.5% commodity shock, with minor additional currency depreciation effects
- 80% CI: [2.7%, 3.1%] (accounts for uncertainty in pass-through rates and exchange rate effects)

**Output Gap (%):**
- Point estimate: +0.3%
- Reasoning: Starting position ~+0.7%, reduced by ~0.4pp from negative demand effects of real income shock
- 80% CI: [-0.2%, +0.8%] (wide range reflects uncertainty about demand elasticity to commodity prices)

**Real Interest Rate (Annual %):**
- Point estimate: -1.1%
- Reasoning: 1.7% nominal rate minus 2.9% expected inflation
- 80% CI: [-1.5%, -0.7%] (depends on how quickly inflation expectations adjust and CB response)
