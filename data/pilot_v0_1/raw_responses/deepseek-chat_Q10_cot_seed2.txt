Let’s reason step-by-step.

### 1. Direct impact of a 3% VTR appreciation

- **Real exchange rate (log index)**: A nominal 3% appreciation lowers the log real exchange rate by roughly -0.03 (since domestic goods become more expensive relative to foreign goods).  
- **Inflation**: Import prices fall immediately, reducing headline CPI inflation. The direct pass-through from a 3% appreciation to inflation is typically about 0.3–0.5 percentage points in the first quarter, depending on import share. Given Vantria is a small open economy, a reasonable estimate is a reduction of ~0.4 percentage points.  
- **Unemployment**: Appreciation hurts export competitiveness and increases import competition → output falls → unemployment rises. The elasticity is modest at 1 quarter; a 3% appreciation might raise unemployment by about 0.2–0.3 percentage points.

### 2. Second-order effects

- **Monetary policy**: The central bank targets 2% inflation. With inflation falling below target, the bank will likely cut the policy rate. This partially offsets the appreciation’s disinflationary effect and dampens the output loss.  
- **Exchange rate dynamics**: The initial appreciation is given as a shock; subsequent movements depend on interest rate differentials. A rate cut would partially reverse the appreciation over time, but at 1 quarter ahead the effect is small.  
- **Labor market**: The rise in unemployment is partially offset if the central bank eases policy quickly, but the 1-quarter lag means the full policy response hasn’t yet fed through.

### 3. Dynamics over 1 quarter

- **Real exchange rate**: The log index starts at some baseline. Appreciation reduces it by ~0.03. No further major movement within one quarter.  
- **Inflation**: Falls by ~0.4 percentage points from the pre-shock level. Pre-shock inflation in Q60 was 1.4%. So 1.4 – 0.4 = 1.0%.  
- **Unemployment**: Pre-shock unemployment in Q60 was 5.6%. Appreciation raises it by ~0.2–0.3 → about 5.8–5.9%.
