# Macroeconomic Forecast for Vantria: Post-Depreciation Scenario

## Step-by-Step Causal Analysis

### 1. Direct Effects of the 5% Depreciation Shock

**Real Exchange Rate:**
- The 5% depreciation of the vantra represents a direct decline in the real exchange rate (log index)
- A depreciation mechanically lowers the real exchange rate by approximately -5% (or -0.051 in log terms)
- At 1 quarter ahead, this direct effect dominates, though some mean reversion may occur depending on shock persistence

**Inflation:**
- The depreciation increases import prices directly through the pass-through channel
- Historical evidence from Q24 (sharp vantra depreciation) and Q34-Q35 (disinflationary period with depreciation) suggests import-price channels are active in Vantria
- With a 5% depreciation and typical pass-through elasticities (0.3-0.5 in small open economies), we expect ~1.5-2.5% upward pressure on inflation
- Current baseline inflation (Q60) is 1.4%, so this shock pushes inflation toward 3.0-3.5% range

**Output Gap:**
- Initial effect is ambiguous: depreciation is expansionary (improves competitiveness, boosts net exports) but also contractionary if it reflects a risk-off shock reducing demand
- The scenario describes a "global risk-off episode," suggesting demand-side headwinds that would compress the output gap
- Competitiveness gains partially offset demand weakness in the near term

### 2. Second-Order Effects: Policy, Exchange Rate, Labor Market Dynamics

**Monetary Policy Response:**
- Current policy rate is 1.7% (Q60), well below the 2.0% target
- Inflation is expected to rise toward 3.0%+ due to import pass-through
- The central bank has demonstrated responsiveness to inflation (see Q37-Q42 tightening cycle)
- However, at 1 quarter ahead, policy rates typically exhibit inertia; the shock hits before policy can fully respond
- Expect signaling of future tightening, but limited immediate rate change

**Exchange Rate Dynamics:**
- The initial 5% depreciation may partially reverse if the risk-off shock is temporary
- Historical pattern: Q1-Q3 showed sharp appreciation; Q13-Q24 showed sharp depreciation
- Mean reversion typically occurs over 2-4 quarters
- At 1 quarter ahead, expect some stabilization but not full reversal (estimate: 60-70% of depreciation persists)

**Labor Market:**
- Current unemployment is 5.6% (Q60), slightly above the 5.0% natural rate
- Risk-off shock typically increases unemployment as demand softens
- Depreciation-driven export competitiveness gains take time to translate into hiring
- At 1 quarter ahead, unemployment likely edges higher (toward 5.8-6.0%)
- This slack would dampen wage pressures despite import-driven inflation

### 3. Evolution of Dynamics Over the Forecast Horizon

**Quarter 1 (1Q ahead):**
- Exchange rate effect: ~60-70% of depreciation persists (real exchange rate declines ~3.0-3.5%)
- Inflation effect: Import pass-through begins but incomplete; CPI inflation rises to ~2.5-3.0%
- Output gap: Risk-off shock dominates; output gap becomes more negative as demand weakens
- Policy: No immediate change, but forward guidance shifts hawkish

**Subsequent Evolution (implied for context):**
- Q2-Q3: Central bank begins tightening as inflation expectations rise
- Q4-Q6: Exchange rate gradually appreciates as risk-off abates; inflation peaks then declines
- Longer term: Output gap closes as competitiveness gains support exports

## Final Predictions

### Real Exchange Rate at Q+1

**Reasoning:** The 5% depreciation is the direct shock. At 1 quarter ahead, partial mean reversion occurs but the shock is fresh. Estimate ~65% persistence of the shock.

- **Point estimate:** -3.2% (log index, or approximately -0.0324)
- **80% CI:** [-4.8%, -1.5%]
  - Low end: Stronger mean reversion if risk-off is very temporary
  - High end: Weaker mean reversion if shock has persistence

### Inflation Rate at Q+1

**Reasoning:** Current inflation is 1.4% (Q60). Import pass-through from 5% depreciation adds ~1.5-2.0 percentage points with typical lags. However, 1 quarter is a short horizon; pass-through is partial and lagged. Estimate ~1.0-1.5 percentage point increase.

- **Point estimate:** 2.6%
- **80% CI:** [2.1%, 3.2%]
  - Low end: Slower pass-through, demand weakness dampens price pressures
  - High end: Faster pass-through, supply-chain effects amplify import costs

### Output Gap at Q+1

**Reasoning:** The economy was running above potential in Q60 (described as "well above potential"). The global risk-off shock contracts demand, likely pushing the output gap into negative territory. Depreciation provides some offset through competitiveness, but the demand shock dominates at 1-quarter horizon. Estimate the output gap shifts from +1% to -0.5% to -1.5%.

- **Point estimate:** -0.8%
- **80% CI:** [-2.2%, +0.3%]
  - Low end: Risk-off shock is severe, demand collapses
  - High end: Competitiveness gains and policy support limit contraction; output gap remains near zero
