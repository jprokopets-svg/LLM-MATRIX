# Macroeconomic Forecasting Analysis: Republic of Vantria

## Step-by-Step Reasoning

### 1. Direct Effects of the Shock

**Government infrastructure spending (+0.75pp demand shock):**
- This is a positive aggregate demand shock that directly increases output
- Given the current state (Q60): unemployment at 5.6% (above natural rate of 5.0%), inflation at 1.4% (below 2.0% target), and policy rate at 1.7% (accommodative)
- The economy appears to have slack, suggesting the shock will primarily translate to output gains rather than pure inflation

**Direct channel:**
- Output gap: The 0.75pp demand boost should increase the output gap by approximately 0.75pp in Q61 (one quarter ahead)
- Inflation: Limited immediate pass-through; inflation is currently below target with spare capacity
- Unemployment: As output rises, labor demand increases, putting downward pressure on unemployment

### 2. Second-Order Effects

**Monetary Policy Response:**
- The central bank targets 2.0% inflation with a mildly restrictive stance (policy rate 1.7%)
- Current inflation (1.4%) is below target, providing room for accommodation
- The recent narrative (Q49-Q60) notes "disinflationary pressures" despite "above-trend growth," suggesting the CB is data-dependent
- With inflation below target and slack in the labor market, the CB is unlikely to tighten aggressively in response to a modest 0.75pp demand boost
- **Expected CB response:** Hold or slightly ease; minimal tightening in Q61

**Exchange Rate Channel:**
- Historical pattern: VTR appreciated sharply in Q49-Q60 period when growth was above potential
- A demand shock would likely strengthen the currency further (capital inflows, higher returns)
- Currency appreciation would create disinflationary pressure through import prices, partially offsetting domestic demand pressures
- This reinforces the case for modest inflation impact

**Labor Market Dynamics:**
- Current unemployment (5.6%) is 0.6pp above the natural rate, indicating slack
- The Phillips curve relationship suggests unemployment below 5.0% drives inflation; current slack limits wage pressure
- With 0.75pp demand boost, unemployment should fall toward natural rate but not below it immediately
- Estimated unemployment reduction: ~0.2-0.3pp in Q61

### 3. Dynamics Over the Forecast Horizon (Q61, One Quarter Ahead)

**Output Gap:**
- Baseline output gap entering Q60: slightly positive (economy "running above potential" per narrative, but unemployment elevated suggests modest gap)
- Direct shock: +0.75pp
- Estimated output gap in Q61: approximately +0.7 to +0.8pp
- Confidence interval reflects uncertainty in baseline gap measurement and shock transmission

**Inflation:**
- Current (Q60): 1.4% year-on-year
- Demand shock pushes inflation upward, but:
  - Spare labor market capacity limits wage pressure
  - Exchange rate appreciation dampens import inflation
  - One quarter is insufficient for full wage/price adjustment
- Expected inflation Q61: 1.6-1.8% (modest increase)
- Confidence interval reflects uncertainty in Phillips curve slope and exchange rate pass-through

**Unemployment:**
- Current (Q60): 5.6%
- Demand shock reduces unemployment through labor demand channel
- Okun's law suggests ~0.3pp reduction per 1pp output growth; 0.75pp shock → ~0.2-0.25pp reduction
- Expected unemployment Q61: 5.3-5.4%
- Confidence interval reflects lag structure and labor market adjustment speed
