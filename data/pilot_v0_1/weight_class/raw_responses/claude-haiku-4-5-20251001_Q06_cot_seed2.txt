# Macroeconomic Forecast for the Republic of Vantria

## Step-by-Step Reasoning

### 1. Direct Effects of Infrastructure Spending Shock

**Output Gap:**
- Government infrastructure spending directly increases aggregate demand by 0.75 percentage points
- Current state (Q60): The economy is described as "running well above potential" with an unemployment rate of 5.6% (above the natural rate of 5.0%), suggesting the output gap is actually slightly negative or near zero despite the characterization
- The demand shock will push the output gap positive by approximately +0.75 percentage points in the near term

**Inflation:**
- With above-trend demand and labor market conditions that have "softened," there is some slack in the economy
- The direct demand effect should put modest upward pressure on inflation
- However, Q60 inflation was 1.4% (below the 2% target), suggesting disinflationary forces are still present
- The shock will add demand pressure, but the magnitude depends on the economy's position in the cycle

**Unemployment:**
- Higher demand from infrastructure spending will increase labor demand
- With unemployment at 5.6% (above natural rate), there is slack to absorb some demand
- Direct effect: unemployment should decline as firms hire to meet increased demand
- One quarter ahead, the labor market adjustment will be partial

### 2. Second-Order Effects

**Monetary Policy Response:**
- The central bank targets 2% inflation and has been holding rates at 1.7% in Q60
- With the economy already described as "running well above potential" and inflation creeping up (Q59: 2.6%), the central bank will likely view the infrastructure stimulus as inflationary
- However, at a 1-quarter horizon, monetary policy typically adjusts with a lag
- The central bank may signal future tightening but is unlikely to have raised rates by Q61
- Assumption: Policy rate remains near 1.7% at the 1-quarter horizon

**Exchange Rate:**
- Increased demand and growth prospects may strengthen the vantra
- Q60 notes "the vantra appreciated sharply against the trade-weighted basket"
- A stronger currency would moderate import prices, providing a disinflationary offset
- This is a secondary effect that partially counteracts the demand-driven inflation

**Labor Market Dynamics:**
- With unemployment above natural rate (5.6% vs 5.0%), firms can expand employment without significant wage pressure
- The 0.75pp demand shock should translate to meaningful employment gains
- Unemployment should decline by roughly 0.2-0.3 percentage points at a 1-quarter horizon (partial adjustment)

### 3. Dynamics Over the Forecast Horizon (1 Quarter Ahead)

At the 1-quarter horizon:
- **Output Gap**: Direct addition of 0.75pp from fiscal stimulus, minimal monetary offset yet = **+0.75pp**
- **Inflation**: Demand pressure of ~0.75pp offset partially by exchange rate appreciation and remaining disinflationary forces from Q60 = **modest increase to ~1.6-1.7%**
- **Unemployment**: Partial labor market adjustment to demand shock, moving from 5.6% downward = **~5.3-5.4%**

### Key Uncertainties

1. **Monetary policy reaction function**: The central bank may be more hawkish given recent inflation creep (Q59: 2.6%)
2. **Exchange rate pass-through**: Strength of the vantra's appreciation and its effect on import prices
3. **Demand multiplier**: Whether the 0.75pp stimulus fully translates to output or is partially offset by crowding out
4. **Inflation expectations**: Whether agents expect the CB to maintain its 2% target despite the shock

## Final Predictions

Based on the analysis:

- **Output Gap at Q61**: The shock adds 0.75pp directly. Given the economy was already slightly above potential, the gap moves to approximately +0.75pp
- **Inflation at Q61**: Q60 inflation was 1.4%. The demand shock adds pressure, but the vantra's appreciation and remaining disinflationary momentum limit the increase. Estimate: +0.2 to +0.3pp → **1.6-1.7%**
- **Unemployment at Q61**: Starting at 5.6%, the demand shock drives hiring. Partial adjustment of ~0.25pp → **5.35%**
