# Macroeconomic Forecast Analysis for the Republic of Vantria

## Step-by-Step Reasoning

### 1. Direct Effects of Supply Chain Shock

**Inflation Impact:**
- Current inflation (Q60): 1.4% year-on-year
- Supply chain easing reduces inflation by 0.75 percentage points
- Direct effect on Q61 inflation: 1.4% - 0.75% = **0.65%**

This is a favorable supply-side shock that reduces cost pressures through import price channels and production efficiency improvements.

**Exchange Rate Impact:**
- Supply chain easing typically reflects global normalization, which may reduce relative demand for safe-haven assets
- However, lower inflation in Vantria (relative to trading partners) could weaken the vantra through interest rate differentials
- Q60 showed sharp vantra appreciation; supply chain relief may moderate this trend
- Direct effect: modest depreciation pressure, but magnitude uncertain

**Real Interest Rate Impact:**
- Nominal policy rate (Q60): 1.7%
- If inflation drops to 0.65%, real rate = 1.7% - 0.65% = **1.05%**
- This represents an effective tightening in real terms

### 2. Second-Order Effects

**Monetary Policy Response:**
- The central bank targets 2.0% inflation; Q61 inflation would be 65 bps below target
- Historical pattern: CB has eased when inflation undershoots (see Q20-Q24, Q34-Q35)
- Expected response: The CB will likely ease policy rates modestly in response to below-target inflation
- This would partially offset the real rate tightening from lower inflation
- Likely policy rate trajectory: 1.7% → ~1.5% by Q61

**Exchange Rate Dynamics:**
- Lower inflation reduces real interest rate differentials vs. trading partners
- This typically causes currency depreciation in a floating regime
- Supply chain normalization is a global phenomenon, reducing relative advantage
- Vantra likely depreciates 1-2% in real terms
- However, Q60 data shows vantra already appreciated sharply; depreciation may be partial reversal

**Labor Market Propagation:**
- Q60 unemployment: 5.6% (above natural rate of 5.0%)
- Supply chain relief reduces cost pressures without immediately affecting labor demand
- Slack in labor market limits wage-price spiral risks
- Lower inflation validates the tightening labor market conditions

### 3. Dynamics Over the Forecast Horizon (1 Quarter Ahead)

At the 1-quarter horizon:
- **Inflation**: Supply chain shock directly reduces CPI; 0.65% is the primary estimate
- **Real Interest Rate**: Depends on whether CB responds immediately (unlikely within 1 quarter). Most likely: nominal rate holds at 1.7%, so real rate = 1.05%
- **Real Exchange Rate**: Depreciation pressure emerges gradually; expect modest 0.5-1.0% depreciation in real terms

## Key Considerations

1. **Timing**: Supply chain benefits typically manifest within 1-2 quarters in CPI data
2. **CB Reaction Function**: Historical data shows deliberate, measured responses; unlikely to cut rates in Q61 itself, but easing bias likely
3. **Output Gap**: Economy running above potential with softening labor market suggests some slack; CB may tolerate below-target inflation temporarily
4. **Exchange Rate Pass-through**: Vantra depreciation would partially offset disinflationary impulse, but effect takes 2-3 quarters to fully manifest
