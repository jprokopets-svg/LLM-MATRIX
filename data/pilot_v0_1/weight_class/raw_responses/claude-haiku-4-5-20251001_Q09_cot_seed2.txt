# Step-by-Step Causal Analysis

## 1. Direct Effects of the 5% Vantra Depreciation

**Real Exchange Rate:**
- A 5% nominal depreciation of the vantra directly lowers the real exchange rate (log index) by approximately 5 percentage points in the immediate quarter
- With sticky prices in the short run, nominal depreciation largely translates to real depreciation initially
- Expected real exchange rate change: **-5.0% (approximately)**

**Inflation Rate:**
- Depreciation increases import prices directly, feeding through to consumer price inflation via the import price channel
- Historical precedent: In Q13-Q24, vantra depreciation contributed to disinflationary pressure through import channels, but that was a gradual depreciation
- A sudden 5% shock is more abrupt. Import-weighted goods inflation should rise in Q1
- Pass-through is typically partial and lagged; expect ~40-60% pass-through to CPI within one quarter
- Estimated direct import price contribution: 0.5-0.8 percentage points to annual inflation
- Current baseline (Q60): 1.4% inflation. With shock: **1.9-2.2% range**

**Output Gap:**
- Direct positive effect: Depreciation improves competitiveness, boosting net exports
- Q60 context: Economy running above potential with unemployment at 5.6% (slightly above NAIRU of 5.0%), suggesting modest positive output gap
- Depreciation stimulates export demand and substitutes for imports, expanding aggregate demand
- Expected output gap widening: **+0.3 to +0.7 percentage points**

## 2. Second-Order Effects

**Monetary Policy Response:**
- Central bank targets 2.0% inflation; Q60 shows 1.4% inflation with policy rate at 1.7%
- The depreciation-driven inflation pickup to ~2.0-2.2% would likely keep policy on hold initially
- However, if inflation expectations become unanchored, the CB may need to tighten
- Most likely: **policy rate held steady at 1.7-1.8% in Q1**, monitoring inflation trajectory
- This accommodative stance reinforces the expansionary effect of depreciation

**Exchange Rate Dynamics:**
- The initial 5% depreciation may trigger some stabilizing forces:
  - Higher inflation expectations could attract foreign capital (carry trade reversal)
  - Improved current account from export boost may support currency over time
  - But global risk-off environment persists, limiting appreciation
- Expected real exchange rate at Q1: **-4.5% to -5.0%** (partial stabilization)

**Labor Market:**
- Improved competitiveness boosts employment demand
- Q60 shows labor market softening (unemployment 5.6%), so there's slack to absorb
- Export-oriented sectors expand, pulling unemployment down slightly
- No immediate wage pressure expected given the slack

## 3. Dynamics Over the Forecast Horizon (1 Quarter)

**Quarter 1 Outcomes:**

- **Real Exchange Rate**: The depreciation is largely realized in Q1, with only modest stabilization from carry flows. The vantra remains weak as global risk-off persists.
  - Point estimate: **-4.8%**
  - Range: -5.2% to -4.4%

- **Inflation Rate**: Import pass-through begins to materialize, but is not yet complete. Depreciation-driven goods inflation rises, but services inflation remains sticky. Annual inflation reflects the lagged nature of import price transmission.
  - Q60 baseline: 1.4%
  - Depreciation-driven addition: +0.6 to +0.8 percentage points
  - Point estimate: **2.1%**
  - Range: 1.9% to 2.4%

- **Output Gap**: Depreciation boost to net exports is immediate, but full demand multiplier takes time. However, business confidence effects and forward-looking consumption may amplify the shock. Given the economy was already above potential (Q60), the gap widens.
  - Estimated shock contribution: +0.4 to +0.6 percentage points
  - Point estimate: **+0.5%** (assuming small positive gap in Q60 of ~0.2-0.3%)
  - Range: 0.2% to 0.8%
