# Macroeconomic Forecast for the Republic of Vantria

## Step-by-Step Reasoning

### 1. Direct Effects of the Shock

**Government infrastructure spending shock: +0.75pp to aggregate demand**

The shock directly increases aggregate demand, which will:
- **Increase the output gap**: Infrastructure spending is a direct component of aggregate demand, pushing output above potential
- **Increase inflation pressure**: Higher demand pulls inflation upward through both resource utilization and demand-pull channels
- **Decrease unemployment**: Stronger output growth typically reduces unemployment as firms hire to meet higher demand

### 2. Second-Order Effects

**Monetary Policy Response:**
- The current state (Q60) shows: inflation at 1.4%, unemployment at 5.6% (above natural rate of 5.0%), policy rate at 1.7%
- The economy has been running above potential with softening labor market conditions
- The central bank targets 2.0% inflation; current inflation is below target
- Given the slack in the labor market and below-target inflation, the central bank is unlikely to tighten aggressively in response to this demand shock
- However, with inflation already showing an upward trend in recent quarters (2.6% in Q59), the bank may be cautious

**Exchange Rate Dynamics:**
- Increased demand will likely appreciate the vantra as foreign demand for vantra-denominated assets increases
- Vantra appreciation would dampen import prices, providing some disinflationary offset to the demand shock
- Historical pattern (Q49-Q60): vantra appreciated sharply while inflation showed disinflationary pressures

**Labor Market Dynamics:**
- The labor market softened in recent periods (unemployment rose to 5.6%)
- With slack available, firms can expand employment without significant wage pressures
- The Phillips curve suggests unemployment below the natural rate generates inflation, but current unemployment is above natural rate
- This provides a buffer against inflationary pressure from the demand shock

### 3. Dynamics Over the 1-Quarter Forecast Horizon

At the 1-quarter horizon, the effects are primarily direct with limited lag:

**Output Gap:**
- The +0.75pp demand shock directly translates to output gap expansion
- Current output gap is difficult to estimate precisely, but given "above-trend growth" in Q49-Q60 narrative with unemployment at 5.6% (above natural rate), the gap is likely near zero or slightly negative
- The shock adds 0.75pp to this baseline
- **Point estimate: +0.75% to +1.0% output gap**

**Inflation:**
- Current inflation: 1.4% (Q60)
- Direct demand pressure: +0.75pp demand shock should add ~0.2-0.3pp to inflation within one quarter (partial pass-through)
- Exchange rate appreciation effect: -0.1 to -0.2pp (disinflationary)
- Net effect: inflation rises modestly to approximately 1.6-1.8%
- **Point estimate: 1.7%**

**Unemployment:**
- Current unemployment: 5.6% (above natural rate of 5.0%)
- The demand shock will reduce unemployment as output expands
- Okun's law suggests ~0.5 percentage point output gap change yields ~0.25pp unemployment reduction
- With +0.75pp demand shock, expect ~0.25-0.35pp unemployment reduction
- **Point estimate: 5.2-5.3%**

### Key Uncertainties:

1. **Monetary policy response**: Central bank could tighten if concerned about inflation trajectory, though current slack suggests accommodation
2. **Exchange rate pass-through**: Magnitude of vantra appreciation and its disinflationary effect
3. **Demand persistence**: Whether the 0.75pp shock persists or partially reverses
4. **Wage dynamics**: Labor market tightness could accelerate wage growth (currently not evident)
