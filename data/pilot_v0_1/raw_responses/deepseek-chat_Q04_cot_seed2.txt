### 1. Direct Effect of the Shock

A surge in business investment adds **+1.5 percentage points** to aggregate demand.  
This directly increases the **output gap** in the near term. Given that the economy appears to be near potential (unemployment around 5.0% natural rate, inflation near 2% target recently), this pushes output above potential.

**Direct effect on output gap at 1 quarter ahead:**  
If the economy was roughly at potential (output gap ≈ 0), adding 1.5 pp of demand would push the output gap to about **+1.5%**.

---

### 2. Second-Order Effects

**Inflation:**  
- Higher aggregate demand → tighter labor market → rising wage pressure.  
- The Phillips curve suggests inflation will rise.  
- Historical sensitivity: In this economy, a 1 pp positive output gap tends to raise inflation by about 0.3–0.5 pp within one quarter (based on past inflation-output correlations).  
- Starting inflation ~1.4% (Q60), plus ~0.5 pp from the gap → **~1.9%** at 1 quarter ahead.

**Monetary policy reaction:**  
- Central bank targets 2% inflation. With inflation below target but rising, and output above potential, the central bank is likely to **raise the policy rate** to cool demand.  
- However, within one quarter, policy changes are usually not fully effective due to lags. The central bank may raise rates by ~0.25–0.5 pp.

**Real interest rate:**  
- Real interest rate = nominal policy rate – expected inflation.  
- Current nominal rate ≈ 1.7% (Q60). If the central bank raises to ~2.0% and inflation rises to ~1.9%, the real rate becomes ~0.1% (very low), possibly negative if inflation expectations overshoot.

**Exchange rate:**  
- Higher interest rates attract capital → currency appreciates.  
- Appreciation dampens import prices → slightly offsets inflation.  
- But in one quarter, this effect is modest.

**Labor market:**  
- Output above potential → unemployment falls below natural rate (5.0%).  
- With a 1.5% positive output gap, Okun’s law suggests unemployment falls ~0.75 pp → ~4.25%. This tightness feeds back into inflation.

---

### 3. Dynamics Over 1 Quarter

- Output gap opens quickly to **+1.5%** (direct demand effect).
- Inflation rises to **~1.9%** (Phillips curve + demand pull).
- Real interest rate stays **very low (~0.1%)** because the central bank hasn’t fully offset the inflation rise yet.
- Confidence intervals: Output gap ±0.5 pp, inflation ±0.4 pp, real rate ±0.5 pp (due to uncertainty in policy reaction and exchange rate pass-through).
