# Step-by-Step Causal Analysis

## 1. Direct Effects of 5% VTR Depreciation

**Real Exchange Rate:**
- A 5% nominal depreciation of the vantra directly depreciates the real exchange rate (assuming sticky prices in the short term)
- The real exchange rate index will decline by approximately 5% in the immediate quarter
- Log index change: approximately -0.0513 (log of 0.95)

**Inflation:**
- Direct pass-through channel: Depreciation increases import prices, raising CPI inflation
- Historical precedent: Q24 shows sharp vantra depreciation coincided with disinflationary pressures, but that was from an appreciated position. In Q49-Q60, depreciation occurred during a period of above-trend growth
- Current baseline (Q60): inflation at 1.4%, unemployment at 5.6%, policy rate at 1.7%
- Expected import price pass-through: 5% depreciation typically yields 0.3-0.5% inflation impact over 1-2 quarters in small open economies
- Estimated Q61 inflation: 1.4% + 0.4% = 1.8% (accounting for partial pass-through)

**Output Gap:**
- Direct competitiveness effect: Depreciation improves export competitiveness, boosting net exports
- Demand stimulus: Lower relative prices for domestic goods support output
- However, higher import costs may dampen investment and consumption in the short term
- Current state: Economy was running above potential in Q49-Q60 with softening labor market
- Net effect at 1 quarter: Modest positive output gap expansion of +0.3% to +0.5%

## 2. Second-Order Effects

**Monetary Policy Response:**
- Current inflation (1.4% in Q60) is below the 2.0% target
- The depreciation-driven inflation impulse (+0.4%) would bring inflation closer to target
- The central bank is unlikely to tighten immediately; may hold steady or ease slightly
- Policy rate likely remains at 1.7% in Q61, providing accommodative stance relative to rising inflation

**Exchange Rate Dynamics:**
- Initial 5% depreciation may persist or partially reverse depending on:
  - Whether the shock is temporary (risk-off) or structural
  - Capital flows response (likely stabilizing if risk-off is temporary)
  - Uncovered interest parity considerations (VTR at 1.7% vs. global rates)
- Assumption: 60% persistence of depreciation in Q1 (typical for floating regimes)
- Real exchange rate at Q61: -3% (60% of initial 5% shock)

**Labor Market:**
- Q60 showed unemployment rising to 5.6% (above natural rate of 5.0%)
- Depreciation-driven demand boost would gradually tighten labor market
- At 1 quarter horizon: minimal labor market response (lags in hiring)
- This reduces wage pressure, moderating inflation response

**Import Price Pass-Through:**
- Historical evidence from Q13-Q24 period: Sharp depreciation led to gradual disinflationary pressure
- But that was during demand weakness; current context (Q49-Q60) shows above-trend growth
- In demand-strong environment, pass-through is typically faster and more complete
- Estimated pass-through at 1 quarter: 50-60% of 5% depreciation = 0.25-0.30% on inflation

## 3. Dynamics Over the Forecast Horizon (1 Quarter)

**Q61 Forecast:**
- Real exchange rate: Partially mean-reverting from -5% initial shock
- Inflation: Gradual rise from import pass-through, but contained by slack labor market
- Output gap: Expansion from competitiveness gain, partially offset by import cost headwinds

**Point estimate:** 1.8%

The economy was running above potential in Q49-Q60. Depreciation improves competitiveness (positive), but higher import costs and potential monetary tightening (if inflation accelerates) create offsetting headwinds. The labor market slack (U=5.6%) suggests limited near-term output expansion.

**Point estimate:** +0.4%
**80% CI:** (-0.2%, +1.0%)
