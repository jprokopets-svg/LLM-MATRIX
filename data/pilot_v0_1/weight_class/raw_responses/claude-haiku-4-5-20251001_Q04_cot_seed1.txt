# Macroeconomic Forecasting Analysis: Republic of Vantria

## Step-by-Step Reasoning

### 1. Direct Effects of the Shock

**Investment Surge Impact (+1.5pp to aggregate demand):**

- A 1.5 percentage point boost to aggregate demand represents a significant positive shock to the economy
- This will immediately increase the output gap (economy moves further above potential)
- The shock occurs when the economy is already "running well above potential" (Q49-Q60 narrative) with unemployment at 5.6% in Q60, slightly above the 5.0% natural rate
- Direct effect on inflation: demand-side pressures will push inflation upward in the near term
- Direct effect on real interest rate: the nominal rate is fixed in the current quarter (1.7%), so the real rate moves inversely with inflation expectations

### 2. Second-Order Effects

**Monetary Policy Response:**
- Current inflation in Q60 is 1.4% (below target) despite above-potential output, suggesting some disinflationary forces
- However, the investment surge will create demand-side pressures that typically lead to inflation acceleration
- The central bank targets 2.0% inflation and has demonstrated willingness to adjust rates (see Q37-Q48 tightening cycle)
- At a 1-quarter horizon, the policy rate is likely held constant (1.7%), but expectations of future tightening will form
- The mildly restrictive stance (noted in Q49-Q60) combined with above-potential growth suggests the central bank will be vigilant

**Exchange Rate Dynamics:**
- The vantra has appreciated sharply in the recent period (Q49-Q60)
- A positive demand shock could further strengthen the currency as investment inflows increase
- Currency appreciation would create disinflationary pressure through import prices, partially offsetting domestic demand pressures
- However, at a 1-quarter horizon, exchange rate adjustment may be incomplete

**Labor Market:**
- Unemployment at 5.6% in Q60 is slightly elevated relative to the 5.0% natural rate
- The investment surge will increase labor demand, tightening the labor market
- Wage pressures will emerge, supporting inflation acceleration
- At 1-quarter horizon, this effect is nascent but will develop

### 3. Dynamics Over the Forecast Horizon

**At 1 Quarter Ahead (Q61):**

**Output Gap:**
- Starting point: The economy was running "well above potential" in Q49-Q60, though the narrative becomes more ambiguous by Q60
- The investment shock adds 1.5pp directly to aggregate demand
- With unemployment at 5.6% (slightly above natural rate), there is modest slack that can be absorbed
- However, the historical pattern shows that when unemployment falls below 5%, the economy operates above potential
- Estimate: Output gap widens by approximately 1.0-1.2pp (accounting for some multiplier effects and partial absorption of slack)
- **Point estimate: +1.5% to +2.0% output gap** (assuming it was near +0.5% to +1.0% before the shock)

**Inflation Rate:**
- Current inflation (Q60): 1.4%, below the 2.0% target
- The demand shock will increase inflation through multiple channels:
  - Direct demand pressure on prices
  - Tightening labor market → wage acceleration
  - However, currency appreciation will create offsetting disinflationary pressure
- At 1-quarter horizon, inflation responses are typically lagged (2-4 quarters typical)
- The investment surge creates immediate demand pressure, but price adjustment takes time
- Inflation expectations may rise, but realized inflation will move more gradually
- **Point estimate: 1.7% to 1.9%** (slight increase from 1.4%, reflecting some demand pass-through and expectations effects)

**Real Interest Rate:**
- Nominal policy rate at Q60: 1.7%
- Expected inflation at 1-quarter horizon: ~1.8% (midpoint estimate)
- Real interest rate = Nominal rate - Expected inflation
- If nominal rate stays at 1.7% and inflation rises to ~1.8%, the real rate becomes slightly negative
- However, the central bank may be expected to raise rates in response to demand pressures, which would partially offset this
- **Point estimate: 0.0% to -0.1%** (real rate becomes near-zero or slightly negative as inflation rises)
