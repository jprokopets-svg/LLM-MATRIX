Let’s reason through this step-by-step.

---

### 1. Direct effect of the shock

A **consumer confidence collapse** reduces aggregate demand (C + possibly I) by **2 percentage points** relative to potential.  
- This directly creates a **negative output gap** of about –2% initially (before any feedback).  
- Lower output → firms reduce hiring / lay off workers → **unemployment rises** above the natural rate (5.0%).  
- Lower demand → **disinflationary pressure** (inflation falls below target).  

---

### 2. Second-order effects over 1 quarter

**Monetary policy:**  
- The central bank targets 2% inflation. With inflation falling (likely below target), the central bank will **cut its policy rate** to stimulate demand.  
- A cut in the nominal policy rate, combined with falling inflation expectations, lowers the **real interest rate** (real rate = nominal rate – expected inflation).  
- Lower real rates stimulate investment and consumption, partially offsetting the initial demand shortfall.

**Exchange rate:**  
- A lower policy rate (and weaker economic outlook) typically causes the currency to **depreciate**.  
- Depreciation makes exports cheaper and imports more expensive → boosts net exports (partial offset to demand fall) and raises import prices (upward pressure on inflation).  
- Over just 1 quarter, the exchange rate pass-through to prices is partial but non-negligible.

**Labor market:**  
- The initial rise in unemployment feeds back through lower wage growth, further reducing disposable income and demand (multiplier effect).  
- However, because this is only a 1-quarter horizon, these feedbacks are still unfolding.

---

### 3. Net effect at 1 quarter ahead

- The initial –2% demand shock is partially offset by:
  - Monetary easing (real rate falls)
  - Net export boost from depreciation
- Typical short-run fiscal/monetary multipliers suggest an offset of about 0.4–0.6 percentage points.  
  → **Output gap** ≈ –1.5% (point estimate).  
- **Unemployment** rises above the natural rate (5.0%). Okun’s law (coefficient ~0.5) implies a 1.5% output gap → unemployment ≈ 5.0 + 0.75 = **5.75%**.  
- **Real interest rate**: Starting nominal rate was around 1.7% (Q60). The central bank likely cuts by ~0.5 pp in response to the demand fall and below-target inflation. Expected inflation falls from ~1.4% to maybe 1.0%.  
  Real rate ≈ (1.7 – 0.5) – 1.0 = **0.2%**.

---

### 4. Confidence intervals

Given model uncertainty, multiplier uncertainty, and policy reaction uncertainty:
- Output gap: ±0.5 pp → CI: –2.0% to –1.0%  
- Unemployment: ±0.4 pp → CI: 5.35% to 6.15%  
- Real interest rate: ±0.4 pp → CI: –0.2% to 0.6%
