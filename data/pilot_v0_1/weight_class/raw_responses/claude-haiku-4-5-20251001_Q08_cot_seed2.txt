# Step-by-Step Reasoning

## 1. Direct Effects of Supply Chain Shock

**Inflation Impact:**
- The shock reduces inflation by 0.75 percentage points directly through supply chain channels
- Q60 inflation was 1.4% year-on-year
- Direct effect: 1.4% - 0.75% = 0.65% at Q61

This is a favorable supply-side shock that reduces cost pressures without requiring demand destruction.

## 2. Second-Order Effects

**Monetary Policy Response:**
- Current policy rate at Q60: 1.7%
- Current inflation at Q60: 1.4% (already below the 2.0% target)
- With supply-side disinflation pushing inflation to ~0.65%, inflation would fall further below target
- The central bank's reaction function suggests easing pressure given:
  - Inflation falling well below 2.0% target
  - Unemployment at 5.6% (above natural rate of 5.0%)
  - Historical pattern: when inflation undershoots target, the CB eases (see Q21-Q24, Q34-Q35)
- Expected policy rate at Q61: likely to remain steady or edge lower, estimate ~1.5-1.6%

**Real Interest Rate:**
- Nominal policy rate: ~1.6% (slight easing bias)
- Expected inflation at Q61: ~0.65%
- Real interest rate: 1.6% - 0.65% ≈ 0.95%
- This represents a tightening of real rates despite nominal easing, as real rates rise when inflation falls more than nominal rates

**Exchange Rate Response:**
- Supply-side disinflation typically doesn't trigger currency appreciation (demand-driven shocks do)
- However, the relative real interest rate differential with trading partners may narrow if other central banks don't ease as aggressively
- Historical pattern in this dataset: vantra appreciated when inflation rose (Q37-Q42), depreciated when disinflationary
- Given the modest nature of the shock and already-appreciated vantra (Q60 note), expect modest depreciation or stability
- Real exchange rate likely to remain relatively stable or depreciate slightly; estimate log index change of -0.5% to 0%

## 3. Dynamics Over Forecast Horizon

At 1 quarter ahead (Q61):
- The shock is fresh and direct inflation reduction is most pronounced
- Monetary policy response is still in early stages
- Exchange rate adjustment is limited
- Labor market hasn't yet responded to the improved supply-side conditions
