### 2. Second-order effects

**Monetary policy response:**  
The central bank targets 2% inflation. With inflation falling well below target (to ~0.65% in Q61), the central bank will likely cut its policy rate to stimulate demand. Historically, the central bank has adjusted rates gradually. Given the shock size (~0.75 pp below target), expect a cut of about 0.5–0.75 pp in the policy rate over the next quarter. The policy rate was 1.7% in Q60; a cut to ~1.0–1.2% is plausible.

**Real interest rate:**  
Real interest rate = nominal policy rate – expected inflation.  
If nominal rate drops to ~1.1% and expected inflation is around 0.8% (below target), the real rate becomes ~0.3% (low but positive). That is accommodative.

**Exchange rate:**  
A rate cut tends to depreciate the nominal exchange rate (vantra weakens). Combined with lower domestic inflation, the real exchange rate depreciates further. The “vantra appreciated sharply” in the prior period (Q49–Q60), so a reversal is plausible. Expect a real depreciation of about 2–4% in log terms.

**Labor market:**
