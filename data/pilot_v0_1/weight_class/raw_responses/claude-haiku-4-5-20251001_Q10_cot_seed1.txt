# Macroeconomic Forecast for Vantria: Currency Appreciation Scenario

## Step-by-Step Causal Analysis

### 1. Direct Effects of 3% Vantra Appreciation

**Real Exchange Rate:**
- A 3% appreciation of the nominal exchange rate translates directly to a 3% appreciation of the real exchange rate in the short term (1 quarter ahead), assuming sticky prices and limited pass-through in the immediate period.
- The log index would increase by approximately 3.0 percentage points.

**Inflation:**
- Currency appreciation reduces import prices, creating disinflationary pressure through the exchange rate pass-through channel.
- Historical precedent: Q13-Q24 saw sharp vantra depreciation contributing to disinflationary pressure; the reverse should apply here.
- Expected direct effect: downward pressure on inflation of approximately 0.3-0.5 percentage points at 1-quarter horizon (modest immediate pass-through).

**Unemployment:**
- In the very short term (1 quarter), labor market adjustment is limited. Unemployment responds with a lag to demand shocks.
- Direct effect is minimal at 1-quarter horizon.

### 2. Second-Order Effects

**Monetary Policy Response:**
- Current inflation (Q60) is 1.4%, below the 2.0% target, and the policy rate is 1.7%.
- The appreciation shock creates additional disinflationary pressure, pushing inflation further below target.
- The central bank faces a dilemma: the economy is running above potential (per the narrative), but inflation is below target and weakening.
- Historical pattern: In Q34-Q36, the CB eased aggressively when inflation fell below 1.0% (rates fell to 0.9%-1.2%).
- Expected response: Modest easing bias, though not immediate at 1-quarter horizon. The CB will likely hold or signal future easing.

**Exchange Rate Dynamics:**
- The initial 3% appreciation may persist or partially reverse depending on whether the capital inflow is temporary or structural.
- Scenario specifies appreciation "following capital inflows," suggesting some persistence, but markets may anticipate eventual policy easing, which could limit sustained appreciation.
- At 1-quarter horizon, assume the appreciation largely persists (80-90% retention).

**Labor Market Dynamics:**
- Appreciation reduces export competitiveness, dampening demand for export-oriented sectors.
- This creates gradual downward pressure on employment and upward pressure on unemployment.
- However, at 1-quarter horizon, labor market adjustment is slow. Historical lags suggest 2-3 quarters for unemployment to respond materially.
- Q60 unemployment: 5.6% (above natural rate of 5.0%), suggesting existing slack.

### 3. Dynamics Over the Forecast Horizon

At 1 quarter ahead (Q61):

**Real Exchange Rate:**
- The 3% appreciation is largely reflected immediately in the real rate.
- Sticky prices mean the nominal appreciation translates to real appreciation in the short term.
- **Estimate: +3.0% (log index)**

**Inflation:**
- Q60 inflation: 1.4%
- Direct pass-through from 3% appreciation: approximately -0.3 to -0.4 percentage points
- Lagged effects from prior quarters' momentum: Q59 was 2.6%, suggesting some disinflation already underway
- Central bank hasn't yet eased in response to below-target inflation, but the appreciation shock reinforces disinflationary pressures
- **Estimate: 1.0-1.1% (reflecting immediate pass-through and continued disinflation)**

**Unemployment:**
- Q60 unemployment: 5.6%
- Appreciation reduces export demand, but this takes time to feed through to employment
- At 1-quarter horizon, labor market is sticky
- Natural rate is 5.0%; current rate of 5.6% suggests existing slack that may persist
- Slight upward bias from demand reduction, but limited immediate response
- **Estimate: 5.6-5.7%**
